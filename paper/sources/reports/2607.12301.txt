## Research Report: XScientist – A Git-Like Research Protocol for Long-Running Autonomous Scientific Discovery

### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The author of this paper is Jixiang Luo, affiliated with jixiangluo85@gmail.com. The work is tied to an open-source project repository maintained on GitHub.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous scientific discovery is experiencing growth, driven by advancements in large language models (LLMs) and agentic AI systems. Existing research, such as "The AI Scientist" [1, 2] and "autoresearch" [3], demonstrates the capability of LLMs to automate various stages of the scientific workflow, including ideation, experiment scripting, data interpretation, manuscript drafting, and self-review. These systems primarily focus on generating plausible scientific manuscripts or conducting specific research tasks.

XScientist distinguishes itself within this landscape by shifting the primary abstraction from producing a final paper to generating a structured, auditable, and forkable research protocol artifact. While previous systems often evaluate their outputs as one-shot paper generators, XScientist addresses the operational challenges inherent in real-world scientific research, which is characterized by its long-running nature, branching explorations, frequent failures, and the necessity of auditable handoffs between automated agents and human operators.

The work builds upon advancements in LLM agents and long-horizon work, drawing inspiration from systems that integrate reasoning and acting (ReAct [5]), utilize verbal feedback loops (Reflexion [6]), explore deliberate branching search (Tree of Thoughts [7]), demonstrate open-ended skill accumulation (Voyager [10]), and manage software engineering tasks through interface design (SWE-agent [8]) or code-space search (AIDE [4]). Multi-agent frameworks like AutoGen [9] also inform the approach to orchestration. XScientist applies these principles of branching and repair to the management of scientific artifacts rather than solely to task execution.

Furthermore, XScientist relates to experiment tracking and versioning tools such as MLflow [11] and DVC [12]. While these tools address aspects of reproducibility for machine learning lifecycles and data/model versioning, XScientist extends this concept by specifying a claim-to-experiment protocol specifically for autonomous research manuscripts. Its Agent-Native Research Artifact (ARA) layer is presented as a git-like object model designed to capture the entire research run, including its evolution and provenance.

Finally, the system integrates principles from automated review and integrity checking research, including reviewer-oriented systems like DeepReviewer [13] and frameworks for evidence-led scrutiny such as Anti-Autoresearch [14]. XScientist incorporates self-review and deterministic integrity forensics, positioning these as conservative safeguards and audit aids rather than definitive proofs of scientific correctness. The paper frames its contribution as a system report and protocol proposal, aiming to provide infrastructure for verifiable autonomous science rather than claiming to replace human scientific review.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind XScientist arises from a recognized operational gap in current autonomous research systems: while these systems can generate plausible scientific outputs, they often lack the mechanisms required for trust, reproducibility, and effective management of the complex, iterative, and often failure-prone nature of real scientific inquiry. The existing paradigm of evaluating autonomous systems as "one-shot paper generators" obscures critical issues related to evidence management, revision control, failure handling, and transparent handoffs.

The core objective of XScientist is to develop a research protocol and operating system that addresses this operational problem by treating each research run as a portable, inspectable artifact rather than merely a final PDF document. This foundational shift is guided by four principles:

1.  **Research runs should be observable:** Every stage of the research workflow should produce structured artifacts that allow for inspection without requiring a full re-execution of the entire process. This promotes transparency and auditability.
2.  **Research runs should be forkable:** The system should enable subsequent agents or human researchers to continue exploration from any specific experiment node within a research lineage, rather than requiring a complete restart from the initial topic or final manuscript. This supports iterative and collaborative research.
3.  **Research claims should be grounded:** Assertions made in a manuscript must be explicitly linked to the specific experiment nodes and evidence artifacts that provide their support. This enhances verifiability and prevents unsupported claims.
4.  **Research automation should expose uncertainty:** Failed branches, reviewer objections, integrity warnings, and blocked quality gates should be preserved and presented as explicit outputs. This contrasts with systems that might hide or prune such "pipeline noise," thereby providing a more realistic and auditable record of the research process.

The cumulative objective is to move autonomous scientific discovery beyond single-run demonstrations towards a more robust, reproducible, reviewable, and forkable research infrastructure. This involves providing a continuously observable pipeline that orchestrates idea generation, experiment execution, manuscript drafting, self-review, repair, quality gating, daemon scheduling, and the creation of reproducibility artifacts. The system aims to export an Agent-Native Research Artifact (ARA) that records an exploration Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG), per-node code and outputs, claim-to-evidence anchors, content hashes, provenance information, and re-execution hooks, making the entire scientific exploration tree inspectable. The paper also aims to specify the ARA layer as a practical interchange format and to document the necessary guardrails for practical autonomous research operation.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

XScientist is designed as a research operating system, orchestrating various stages of scientific discovery through a structured protocol and a set of core components. The system's architecture is summarized in Figure 1, depicting a control plane, a research loop, and an artifact and verification plane.

#### 4.1 Execution Modes

The system offers three primary execution modes:
*   `run project.py`: Executes a complete project from a specified topic or idea.
*   `continuous paper generator.py`: Handles batch and continuous generation of papers across a range of ideas and manuscript types.
*   `continuous research daemon.py`: Operates as a long-lived scheduling loop, managing source queues, providing dashboards and reports, and allowing for operator control.

#### 4.2 Pipeline Artifacts

Throughout its operation, XScientist generates structured JSON and Markdown artifacts. These include idea cards, research plans, experiment registries, reviewer findings, repair plans, manuscript candidates, quality reports, integrity forensics reports, source provenance, and handoff briefs. These artifacts are designed to be stable interfaces for audit and continuation, explicitly prioritized over transient console outputs.

#### 4.3 Core Components

The system is modular, with components that can function independently or within the full pipeline:
*   **Ideation and Planning:** This layer converts topics and source queues into ranked candidate ideas and detailed research plans. Plans delineate tasks, expected outputs, acceptance criteria, baselines, and execution policies.
*   **Experiment Execution:** Experiments are conducted via a tree-search-style loop. The system records code, standard output/error, metrics, plots, and failure states. Failed branches are considered valuable evidence, providing context for subsequent repairs or modifications to claims.
*   **Writing and Review:** The writing layer produces LaTeX manuscripts along with structured metadata. The review layer employs LLM-based and VLM-assisted techniques to identify issues, prioritize repairs, and execute various reviewer-oriented strategies (e.g., novelty, rigor, clarity, reproducibility checks).
*   **Indexing and Management:** This layer provides interfaces (e.g., boards) for tracking generated papers, review issues, repair priorities, process alignment, and evolution signals, facilitating ongoing operational management.

#### 4.4 Operating Assumptions

XScientist assumes that autonomous research should be subject to explicit budgets and review boundaries. It allows for long-running operations but incorporates mechanisms for planned interruption, such as when sample gates fail, truth contracts are violated, integrity forensics reports critical flags, or an operator pauses the daemon. These stopping states are treated as valid and informative outcomes.

#### 4.5 Agent-Native Research Artifacts (ARA) Protocol

The central interface of XScientist is the Agent-Native Research Artifact (ARA), designed to be machine-readable and facilitate interoperability. An ARA is structured as a directory rooted by `manifest.json` and `exploration_graph.json`.

*   **Exploration DAG:** Each ARA contains an exploration Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) where nodes represent concrete states such as experiments, repairs, failures, ablations, or manuscript candidates. Edges denote the evolutionary progression. The DAG is validated to be directed and acyclic and can be rendered as an HTML visualization, turning each paper into a "science exploration tree." This tree intentionally includes failed branches and bug states, providing a complete and auditable history.
*   **Content Hashing and Provenance:** ARA utilizes content hashes for node payloads (code, metrics) and manifest revisions. Forked runs maintain parent pointers, linking a child research run to its originating ARA and node. This creates a commit-like substrate for research state, allowing for diffing, logging, forking, and freezing.
*   **Claim Anchoring:** The writing stage can insert `\claimref` markers within the manuscript, creating invisible, two-way links between quantitative or evidence-sensitive claims and the specific experiment nodes that support them. A claim registry summarizes this coverage for quality gates and reviewers.
*   **Operational Verbs:** A companion command-line interface (CLI) provides verbs such as `inspect`, `exec`, `fork`, `freeze`, `validate`, `verify`, `diff`, `log`, and `refs` for interacting with ARAs.
*   **ARA Directory Contract:** The minimal ARA contract requires `manifest.json` and `exploration_graph.json`. Common optional files include `nodes/<id>/` (containing code, logs, metrics, plots, environment descriptors), `claims/` (claim-to-node anchors), `verify/` (re-execution reports), and `pipeline/` (mirrored planning, review, repair artifacts). The file-based nature of the protocol is chosen to ease archiving, diffing, and consumption by external tools, similar to how `git` repositories function.

#### 4.6 Quality and Integrity Controls

XScientist integrates several deterministic and auditable quality and integrity controls:
*   **Self-Review and Repair:** The system performs LLM/VLM-based reviews, extracts structured issues, prioritizes repairs, and tracks their resolution, including whether changes introduce regressions. Repair attempts are recorded as traces, linking issues to modifications and reruns.
*   **Truth Contracts:** These translate research plans into explicit constraints, covering objective facts, comparability, artifact binding, branch-state rules, and value guardrails. They act as concrete conditions checked by gates or reviewers.
*   **Sample Gates:** Before full generation, sample gates ensure that preliminary tasks (e.g., small planned experiments) have completed records, budget audits, and passing acceptance checks. This prevents scaling flawed plans into full papers.
*   **Deterministic Integrity Forensics:** Inspired by Anti-Autoresearch, this pass creates a span-anchored ledger from the manuscript source and applies model-free consistency checks. It flags unsupported strong wording, arithmetic drift, citation placeholders, and evidence-ledger gaps, with hard findings potentially blocking submission readiness. Its deterministic nature ensures repeatable results.
*   **Decision Logs and Gate Preconditions:** The system records decision logs for workflow strategy and model choices. Gate preconditions are checked before expensive or high-risk stages to ensure adherence to specified workflow and quality modes.

#### 4.7 Long-Running Daemon Operation

The daemon mode treats research as a continuous operation. It features source queues, source health tracking, dashboard serving, daily reports, handoff briefs, pause/resume controls, source boosting and cooldown mechanisms, and failure protection. This facilitates supervised autonomy, allowing operators to interrupt, inspect, and redirect the research process, and enabling feedback loops to influence scheduling and execution policies based on ongoing performance.

#### 4.8 Reproducibility and Release Workflow

XScientist separates source code from generated research outputs by default. For reproducibility, a report should cite the public repository URL, the exact commit hash, configuration, model choices, output directory, and the ARA root used. The recommended workflow involves running a project under an explicit output root, inspecting the generated artifacts, verifying ARA nodes, checking claim coverage, and packaging the manuscript with artifact references and commit hashes.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents XScientist as a functional system and a specified protocol designed to address the operational challenges in autonomous scientific discovery. The primary findings are detailed through the system's architecture, its core components, and the capabilities it provides:

*   **Realization of a Long-Running Research Operating System:** The system demonstrates the design and implementation of an operating system for autonomous research. This system orchestrates the entire research pipeline from ideation to review and repair as a continuously observable process. It moves beyond single-run script execution to support long-term, iterative, and branching scientific inquiry under operator supervision.
*   **Specification of the Agent-Native Research Artifact (ARA) Protocol:** The paper defines the ARA layer as a practical, file-based interchange format for agent-native research state. This includes the conceptualization and implementation of an exploration Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) that records all experimental, repair, and failure nodes, thereby making the scientific process inspectable as an exploration tree. The ARA also incorporates content hashing, provenance tracking, and explicit claim-to-evidence anchoring through `\claimref` markers, linking manuscript assertions directly to supporting experimental data. The availability of CLI tools for interacting with ARAs (e.g., `fork`, `diff`, `verify`) demonstrates the operationalization of this protocol.
*   **Integration of Practical Guardrails and Quality Controls:** XScientist incorporates a suite of quality and integrity controls critical for trustworthy autonomous research. These include an LLM/VLM-assisted self-review and repair subsystem that traces repair attempts; truth contracts that formalize research plans into verifiable constraints; sample gates to prevent premature scaling of flawed ideas; and deterministic integrity forensics that perform model-free consistency checks on manuscripts. The system also implements decision logs and gate preconditions to ensure adherence to workflow strategies and quality modes. These features collectively contribute to making risk states explicit and reproducible, enhancing the audibility of generated research.
*   **Demonstration of Daemon-based Operation:** The paper details the design and functionality of a long-running daemon mode, which allows for continuous scheduling of research sources, tracking source health, providing dashboards, and enabling operator controls. This demonstrates an approach to managing autonomous research as an ongoing, supervised operation, with feedback loops influencing future scheduling and execution policies.
*   **Contrast with One-Shot Generation:** The paper implicitly finds that an artifact-centered, git-like workflow, as implemented in XScientist, provides distinct advantages over conventional one-shot paper generators. These advantages include preserved failure handling, node-level forkability, explicit claim grounding, structured review, and daemon-based operations, addressing the limitations of prior approaches.
*   **Availability of Open-Source Implementation:** The project repository serves as a direct artifact of the findings, containing the running implementation, ARA protocol schemas, command-line tools, examples, documentation, and the source for the report itself. This makes the described system and protocol accessible for inspection, replication, and further development.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

XScientist presents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous scientific discovery by addressing the critical issues of trust, reproducibility, and operational management. Its potential impact spans several dimensions:

*   **Enhanced Trust and Auditability:** By fundamentally re-framing the output of autonomous research from a static PDF to a dynamic, inspectable Agent-Native Research Artifact (ARA), XScientist directly tackles the problem of trust. Researchers and reviewers can trace the entire exploration history, including failed experiments and repair loops, providing a transparent audit trail that is often absent in current AI-generated outputs. This could increase confidence in the scientific rigor of autonomously produced work.
*   **Improved Reproducibility and Verifiability:** The ARA protocol, with its claim anchoring, content hashing, provenance tracking, and re-execution hooks, offers a robust framework for reproducibility. Claims within a manuscript can be directly linked to their supporting evidence, enabling more precise verification. The ability to fork from any node and re-execute experiments can significantly streamline the process of validating or building upon autonomous research.
*   **Foundation for Collaborative Autonomous Science:** The git-like nature of the ARA protocol, allowing for diffing, logging, and forking, establishes a potential standard for interoperability between different autonomous research systems and human scientists. This could foster a more collaborative ecosystem where systems can exchange research state, build on each other's explorations, and subject findings to automated, structured review.
*   **Safer Development and Deployment of Autonomous AI:** The integrated quality and integrity controls—such as truth contracts, sample gates, and deterministic integrity forensics—act as crucial safeguards. By explicitly exposing uncertainties, failures, and potential risks, XScientist contributes to a more responsible approach to autonomous research, preventing the silent propagation of errors or unsupported claims. This is particularly important for high-stakes scientific domains.
*   **Paradigm Shift in Evaluation:** The system proposes an artifact-centered evaluation strategy, shifting the focus from merely judging the final manuscript to assessing the quality and inspectability of the entire research process and its generated artifact trail. This could lead to more holistic and rigorous evaluations of autonomous research capabilities.
*   **Facilitating Research and Development of Autonomous Agents:** For developers of autonomous agents, XScientist provides a structured environment that records detailed traces of agent behavior, decisions, and outcomes. This rich operational data can be invaluable for debugging, improving, and optimizing agent architectures and strategies over long horizons.
*   **Open-Source Contribution:** The open-source availability of the implementation, protocol schemas, and tools lowers the barrier for adoption and further development by the broader research community. This can accelerate the evolution of standards and best practices for verifiable autonomous scientific discovery.
