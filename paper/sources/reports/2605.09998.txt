## Research Paper Report: Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents

This report provides a detailed analysis of the research paper "Continual Harness: Online Adaptation for Self-Improving Foundation Agents," covering its authors, context within the research landscape, objectives, methodology, findings, and significance.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a gap in the development of agentic harnesses for embodied agents, drawing parallels with and distinguishing itself from existing work in large language model (LLM) agents, reinforcement learning, and in-context learning.

Agentic harnesses, which provide foundation models with tools, memory, and planning capabilities, have become established infrastructure for tasks such as autonomous coding (e.g., Claude Code, OpenHands, OpenClaw) and assistant functions. However, the authors note that an equivalent system for embodied agents operating in complex, long-horizon, and partially observable environments, such as role-playing games (RPGs), has been absent. Prior work, including the PokeAgent Challenge, indicated that frontier vision-language models made minimal progress on RPG gameplay without domain-specific scaffolding. The authors' preceding "Gemini Plays Pokémon" (GPP) project demonstrated that human-supervised refinement could address this scaffolding challenge.

The concept of self-improving agents is a related area of research, with methods like prompt optimization (e.g., GEPA, Meta-Harness) and reflective self-improvement (e.g., Reflexion, Self-Refine) aiming to enhance agent performance. These approaches typically involve optimizing harness components or reflecting on trajectories between episodes, often requiring episodic resets. Continual Harness differentiates itself by performing online, reset-free adaptation of the *full* harness state (system prompt, sub-agents, skills, and memory) within a single continuous episode, using partial trajectory windows. This online adaptation aligns with the emerging field of reset-free reinforcement learning, which addresses environments where resets are costly or unavailable.

Furthermore, this work builds upon advancements in in-context learning, where LLMs implicitly improve and perform structured multi-call reasoning over context. Continual Harness formalizes this by explicitly writing structured edits to the harness state. The use of process reward models, which provide more granular feedback than sparse episode rewards, in the co-learning loop also connects to recent efforts in fine-grained reward signaling for LLMs. Overall, the paper positions Continual Harness as a framework that integrates and extends these diverse research threads to create adaptable and self-improving foundation agents for embodied tasks.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop "Continual Harness," a reset-free framework that enables self-improving foundation agents to adapt and refine their operational scaffolding online within a single continuous run. This objective is driven by several motivations rooted in observed limitations and prior successes.

One key motivation stems directly from the authors' "Gemini Plays Pokémon" (GPP) project. In GPP, human-supervised, iterative refinement of an agent's harness (comprising tools, memory, and planning) enabled AI systems to achieve significant milestones, including completing Pokémon Blue, Pokémon Yellow Legacy on hard mode, and Pokémon Crystal without losing a battle. Notably, during the harder stages of Yellow and Crystal, the model itself began to iterate on its strategies by leveraging long-context memory. This "emergent self-improvement" signaled the potential for automating the harness refinement process, thereby removing the human-in-the-loop.

Another motivation addresses the functional gap for embodied agents. While agentic harnesses have become standard for autonomous coding agents, an equivalent system capable of handling the long-horizon, partially observable decision-making inherent in embodied environments (such as navigating game worlds, interacting with NPCs, and managing inventory in RPGs) has been lacking. Existing approaches demonstrated that without domain-specific scaffolding, frontier vision-language models made little progress on complex RPGs. Continual Harness aims to bridge this gap by starting from a minimal environment interface and allowing the agent to construct and refine its own scaffolding.

The research also seeks to overcome limitations of existing prompt-optimization and self-improvement methods. Many of these approaches rely on episodic resets between updates, which can be inefficient or infeasible in environments where resets are costly or unavailable. Continual Harness is motivated by the need for an online, mid-episode adaptation mechanism that allows self-improvement to continue without restarting the entire process. This reset-free paradigm also enables the agent to address failure modes that manifest deep within long episodes, which are inaccessible to reset-based methods.

Ultimately, the objectives include enabling agents to achieve performance comparable to hand-engineered expert systems, but starting from minimal knowledge and without requiring extensive manual domain scaffolding. This involves developing a robust mechanism for the agent to refine its system prompt, sub-agents, skill library, and memory based on its ongoing trajectory data. Furthermore, the work aims to establish a co-learning loop where an open-source model's weights and the harness state are jointly updated in a continual, online fashion, demonstrating sustained in-game progress.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of Continual Harness centers on a two-loop architecture that enables online, reset-free self-improvement of an embodied agent's operational harness, and extends to co-learning with an underlying foundation model.

**4.1 Embodied Agent Environment and Interface**
The agent interacts with its environment, specifically Pokémon Red and Emerald, through a minimal interface. At each timestep ($t$), the agent receives a frame observation ($o_t$, a rendered image) and an ASCII text map ($m_t$). The text map describes visible tiles, walkable positions, NPCs, and the player's location, compensating for known limitations of vision-language models in fine-grained spatial reasoning. It does not provide walkthroughs, objective lists, or global map information. Actions ($a_t$) are selected from a fixed set of button inputs (e.g., UP, DOWN, A, B). The environment is partially observable, as internal states like NPC intent or full battle mechanics are not directly exposed. The primary evaluation metric is cumulative button presses to reach predefined milestones.

**4.2 Agentic Harness (H)**
The core scaffolding, denoted as $H$, comprises four components:
*   **System Prompt ($p$):** Instructions and strategic guidance provided to the model.
*   **Sub-agents ($G$):** Specialized modules invoked for specific tasks (e.g., battle strategy, puzzle solving).
*   **Skills ($K$):** Reusable routines, including pre-built primitives (e.g., `press_buttons`, `get_game_state`) and new skills authored during play.
*   **Memory ($M$):** A persistent knowledge store for accumulating facts, strategies, and observations.

The harness exposes a fixed set of meta-tools (e.g., `define_agent`, `run_code`, `process_memory`) that allow the agent to edit $p, G, K, M$ in place. A minimalist harness ($H_{min}$) provides only the raw environment interface and a generic prompt. A hand-engineered expert harness ($H_{expert}$) pre-populates all components with domain-specific knowledge. Continual Harness ($H_{CH}$) starts from $H_{min}$ and develops its components via an automated Refiner.

**4.3 Two-Loop Architecture for Harness Refinement**
The system operates with an inner and outer loop:
*   **Inner Loop (Agent Step):** The foundation model ($M$) wrapped by the current harness ($H_t$) receives observation $s_t = (o_t, m_t)$ and the trajectory history, then produces an action $a_t$.
*   **Outer Loop (Harness Refinement):** This loop automates the refinement process. Every $F$ steps, after an initial warm-up period of $W$ steps, an LLM acting as the "Refiner" reads a window of the recent trajectory ($\tau_{t-F:t}$).
    *   **Failure Signature Identification:** The Refiner identifies patterns indicative of failure, such as navigation loops, failed tool calls, stalled objectives, or missed exploration opportunities.
    *   **CRUD Edits:** Based on identified failures and the trajectory window, the Refiner generates Create, Read, Update, and Delete (CRUD) edits across the four harness components:
        *   **System Prompt:** Rewrites the prompt ($p$) to address observed shortcomings.
        *   **Sub-agents:** Creates new sub-agents for recurring multi-step patterns, modifies existing ones to correct failures, and deletes unproductive ones.
        *   **Skills:** Codifies successful action sequences into new skills and repairs executable code that previously raised exceptions.
        *   **Memory:** Adds new entries to fill knowledge gaps, updates stale information, and adjusts the importance of memories based on the agent's progress.
    *   **Online Application:** These updates are applied to the harness state ($H_t$) in place, resulting in $H_{t+1} = H_t \oplus \Delta$. The agent continues execution without resetting, ensuring that refinement information accumulates monotonically throughout the episode.

**4.4 Continual Model-Harness Co-Learning Loop**
This extended outer loop enables an open-source model to learn and improve alongside the refining harness.
*   **Warm-up Stages:** An open-source model (Gemma-4 variants) is initially primed through:
    1.  **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Using LoRA on trajectories generated by a frontier model (Gemini-3.1-pro) operating with Continual Harness.
    2.  **Offline Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Generating candidate completions for states in the teacher-visited set, scoring them with a per-step oracle, and updating the policy.
*   **Online Co-Learning Iterations:** Each iteration consists of:
    1.  **Rollout:** The current policy ($\pi_{\theta_k}$) of the open-source model executes within the live-refining harness ($H_t$) for $K=256$ steps.
    2.  **Process Reward Model (PRM) Scoring:** A pairwise PRM (Gemini-3-flash-preview) scores each transition within a sliding window, based on trajectory progress, action correctness, reasoning quality, and format compliance.
    3.  **Teacher Relabeling:** Low-reward windows from the rollout are relabeled by a frontier teacher (Gemini-3.1-pro).
    4.  **Soft SFT Update:** The open-source model's weights ($\theta_k$) are updated via soft SFT on the relabeled shard, producing $\theta_{k+1}$.
*   **Reset-Free Training:** The emulator state at the end of iteration $k$ is saved and loaded as the starting state for iteration $k+1$. This allows the agent's in-game progress and the harness's accumulated refinements to persist across training iterations. This loop facilitates co-adaptation, where the refined harness influences the model's trajectories, and the model's gameplay surfaces new failure modes for further harness refinement.

**4.5 Experimental Setup**
Experiments were conducted on Pokémon Red and Emerald. Various harness conditions were tested: $H_{min}$, $H_{expert}$, and three variants of $H_{CH}$ (from scratch, bootstrap frozen, bootstrap updating). Gemini 3 variants (Pro, Flash, Flash-Lite) were used for primary evaluations, and Gemma-4 models for open-source transfer. Multiple seeds were used for experiments.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings related to the capabilities of Continual Harness, its effectiveness in closing the performance gap to expert systems, its dependence on model capability, and its utility in co-learning with open-source models.

**5.1 Gemini Plays Pokémon (GPP) Achievements and Emergent Self-Improvement**
The GPP project, a predecessor to Continual Harness that involved human-supervised harness refinement, successfully completed multiple Pokémon RPGs. These achievements include Pokémon Blue (May 2025), Pokémon Yellow Legacy on hard mode (August 2025), and Pokémon Crystal without a lost end-game battle (November 2025). This marks the first AI system to complete multiple Pokémon RPGs. A significant observation from GPP was the emergence of self-improvement: in the later, more challenging stages of Yellow and Crystal, the model itself began to iterate on its own strategy using long-context memory. Examples included developing named multi-stage battle strategies ("Operation Zombie Phoenix") and authoring explicit truth-table representations for puzzles. Quantitative analysis of GPP's Yellow Legacy runs showed that CRUD operations on skill and sub-agent definitions persisted throughout the runs, rather than converging, and were concentrated on a small subset of navigation and battle components. This demonstrated continuous adaptation.

**5.2 Continual Harness Closes Gap to Hand-Engineered Harness**
On both Pokémon Red and Emerald, Continual Harness ($H_{CH}$) substantially reduced the cumulative button-press cost for every monitored milestone compared to the minimalist baseline ($H_{min}$). Furthermore, $H_{CH}$ recovered a majority of the efficiency gap between $H_{min}$ and the hand-engineered expert harness ($H_{expert}$), despite starting from a raw interface without curated knowledge, hand-crafted tools, or domain scaffolding. The remaining gap to $H_{expert}$ was primarily observed in dialogue-heavy gym interiors and complex multi-turn battle strategies. The "bootstrap-updating" variant of $H_{CH}$ on Red demonstrated greater efficiency than the "from-scratch" variant at every milestone. This indicates that the refinement signal compounds within an episode, allowing a harness refined in a previous run to accelerate subsequent runs even if the game state resets.

**5.3 Continual Harness Gain Depends on Model Capability**
The effectiveness of Continual Harness was observed to scale with the capability of the underlying foundation model. On Pokémon Emerald, using Gemini 3.1 Pro, $H_{CH}$ was strictly Pareto-dominant over $H_{min}$. For instance, from-scratch $H_{CH}$ achieved 100% of milestones with a median cost of $130, compared to $H_{min}$ reaching 98% for $215, representing approximately a 40% cost reduction without loss of completion. The two bootstrap variants on Pro achieved 96–100% of milestones at $110–$140. In contrast, with Gemini 3.1 Flash, the harness benefit exhibited high variance. With Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, every Continual Harness variant underperformed the minimalist baseline, reaching only 3–13% of milestones at comparable or higher costs, whereas $H_{min}$ reached 20%. These results indicate that a minimum level of model capability is necessary for the refinement loop to effectively utilize and benefit from the harness components.

**5.4 Open-Source Students Co-Learn with a Refining Harness**
The research demonstrated that an open-source model (Gemma-4) could improve its gameplay through a self-refining harness using a reset-free training paradigm. After initial priming stages (supervised fine-tuning and offline GRPO), which alone did not yield significant milestone advancement, the online co-learning loop drove sustained in-game milestone progress on Pokémon Red. This progress was observed from both the beginning of the game and from mid-game checkpoints, suggesting the training signal generalizes across different stages of the game. The process involved the model's rollouts through the refining harness being scored by a process reward model, low-reward windows being relabeled by a frontier teacher (Gemini-3.1-pro), and the model then being updated via soft SFT. This loop facilitated continual model-harness co-learning, where the refined harness shaped the model's trajectories, and the model's gameplay generated new failure modes for subsequent refinement cycles.

**5.5 Skills Measurably Self-Improve Toward an Oracle**
Analysis of refined navigation skills, measured by their path cost relative to a Dijkstra oracle, provided direct evidence of skill self-improvement. While $H_{min}$ never invoked a navigation skill, every Continual Harness condition accumulated hundreds of invocations over a 24-hour run. In from-scratch runs, the path-cost deficit decreased from a substantial penalty at the start to single digits relatively early in the run, and remained low. This improvement occurred in-loop and in a reset-free manner, with failures from earlier invocations diagnosed by the Refiner and the affected skills repaired within the same episode before later invocations. The bootstrap-updating variant, inheriting a refined skill set, matched or outperformed the bootstrap-frozen variant, indicating that continued in-episode refinement provides additional value beyond inherited knowledge. The refinement loop was observed to triage, prioritizing repairs for actively used skills and tolerating regressions for unused ones.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Continual Harness represents a notable step in the field of autonomous agents, particularly for embodied scenarios, with several potential impacts.

Firstly, this work addresses a recognized challenge in deploying foundation models in complex, embodied environments. By demonstrating that an AI system can complete multiple full-length RPGs through automated harness refinement, it provides a functional framework for long-horizon, partially observable decision-making, which has been an area where LLM agents have historically struggled without extensive manual scaffolding. This may reduce the need for human-engineered domain-specific knowledge and tools, making the deployment of AI agents more scalable and efficient across diverse, complex tasks.

Secondly, the introduction of a reset-free, online adaptation mechanism is significant. Unlike many existing prompt-optimization and self-improvement techniques that require episodic resets, Continual Harness allows agents to continuously learn and refine their operational parameters within a single, ongoing run. This is crucial for real-world applications such as robotics, long-running operational tasks, or scientific experiments, where environment resets are costly, impractical, or impossible. The ability of the refinement process to accumulate knowledge and address failure modes that manifest deep within an episode further enhances its practical applicability.

Thirdly, the co-learning loop, which integrates an open-source model with a continually refining harness, opens new avenues for general-purpose embodied AI. The demonstration that an open-source model can achieve sustained in-game progress through this mechanism suggests a pathway for building advanced, adaptable agents without exclusive reliance on proprietary frontier models. This could contribute to the democratization of research and development in embodied AI.

Finally, the detailed attribution of gains to specific harness components (skills, sub-agents, memory, and prompt) and the quantitative evidence of skill self-improvement provide insights into how LLMs can develop and debug their own operational capabilities. This research provides a foundation for future work on explicit reuse priors, more sophisticated deletion policies for unused components, and comparative studies between reset-free and traditional reinforcement learning paradigms. The framework also implicitly validates the capability of large language models to perform complex meta-reasoning, tool creation, and self-correction in interactive settings.
