## Research Report: Regimes: An Auditable, Held-Out-Gated Improvement Loop Demonstrated on LongMemEval with ActiveGraph

This report provides a detailed analysis of the paper "Regimes: An Auditable, Held-Out-Gated Improvement Loop Demonstrated on LongMemEval with ActiveGraph" by Yohei Nakajima.

### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The author of this research paper is Yohei Nakajima, affiliated with Untapped Capital and activegraph.ai.

### 2. Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned at the intersection of several developing areas within artificial intelligence research, specifically focusing on autonomous agents and their improvement mechanisms. It draws from advancements in self-improving agents (e.g., SICA, GRASP, Reflexion, ExpeL), held-out prompt and program optimization (e.g., DSPy), executable failure-mode analysis (e.g., MAST, AgentDebug), and long-context memory benchmarks (e.g., LongMemEval).

The paper distinguishes its approach from concurrent work like GRASP by emphasizing a "runtime story" rather than primarily an "algorithmic self-improvement method." While GRASP focuses on gating natural-language skills in structured procedural environments, Regimes concentrates on an event-sourced substrate to facilitate auditable, reproducible improvement loops for executable patches at typed pipeline seams, particularly targeting evidence reconciliation in long-context memory tasks. The paper shares with DSPy and GRASP the foundational concept of held-out validation for candidate improvements, acknowledging this as prior art rather than a novel contribution.

Furthermore, Regimes diverges from existing failure taxonomies such as MAST and AgentDebug by proposing an *executable* taxonomy. This means that diagnosed failure modes are directly mapped to specific editable parts of the agent's pipeline (action seams), rather than merely labeling the failures for post-hoc analysis. The underlying ActiveGraph runtime builds upon prior work by Nakajima (2026) regarding event-sourced systems for auditable agentic systems, positioning Regimes as an empirical demonstration of the capabilities enabled by such an architecture for self-improvement.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed challenges in building trustworthy autonomous improvement loops for agents. Current approaches often involve external scaffolding, which results in unlogged failures, unreproducible diagnoses, and improvement decisions stored in separate databases rather than within the agent's own historical context. This friction undermines the trust and transparency required for robust self-improvement.

The central objective is to demonstrate that an event-sourced agent runtime can mitigate these issues, transforming controlled improvement into an intrinsic workflow. This is achieved through several specific commitments:

1.  **Auditable and Reproducible Process:** To ensure that all aspects of the improvement process—failures, diagnoses, candidate repairs, gate outcomes, and promotion/discard decisions—are recorded as events in an append-only log. This enables exact replay of runs and provides an auditable history, distinct from anecdotal claims based on external databases. ActiveGraph is presented as the enabling substrate for this auditable property.
2.  **Held-Out-Gated Promotion:** To implement a rigorous validation process where any proposed repair is only retained if it demonstrates improvement on held-out examples, beyond merely performing well on the examples used for its generation. This is intended to prevent overfitting and ensure genuine generalization.
3.  **Target-Agnostic Design:** To engineer an improvement loop whose core logic (diagnosis, gating, rotation) can function across different agent tasks through a common interface, requiring only task-specific components (evaluator, editable pipeline parts, failure labels) to be swapped in.
4.  **Addressing Specific Agent Failures:** To apply and demonstrate the Regimes loop on the LongMemEval-S benchmark, specifically targeting the dominant failure mode identified as "reconciliation" or "assemble-internal." This refers to instances where relevant evidence is present in the assembled context, yet the agent's reader component fails to process it correctly, leading to an incorrect answer.
5.  **Prompt as a Discovery Instrument:** To reframe promoted prompt repairs not as the ultimate solution, but as "high-bandwidth probes." The motivation is that successful repairs, and their specific failure cases, can reveal underlying evidence-use behaviors that should ultimately be formalized into guarded, deterministic operators, moving beyond coarse prose rules.

In essence, the research aims to provide a robust, transparent, and generalizable framework for autonomous agent improvement, with a particular focus on the underlying system architecture as a key enabler.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The Regimes platform is designed to implement a controlled, auditable improvement loop through a structured approach encompassing taxonomies, a defined control flow, and a target-agnostic interface, all built upon an event-sourced runtime.

#### 4.1. The Regimes Platform

The platform defines three distinct categorizations:
*   **Question type:** Categories from the LongMemEval benchmark (e.g., temporal-reasoning, knowledge-update).
*   **Failure regime:** Diagnosed reasons for agent failure (e.g., `budget-truncation` for evidence dropped, `assemble-internal` for evidence present but incorrectly used by the reader, `retrieval-signal-gap` for insufficient scoring of relevant turns).
*   **Action seam:** The specific part of the agent's pipeline that the loop is permitted to modify (e.g., `score-transform` for re-weighting retrieval scores, `assembly-transform` for reordering turns, `reader-prompt-transform` for editing the reader's prompt). These are executable patch objects.

A fixed **regime-to-seam mapping** serves as the core heuristic, routing each diagnosed failure regime to a specific action seam. For example, `assemble-internal` failures are routed to `reader-prompt-transforms`, as these are the only interventions designed to alter how the reader interprets available evidence. Failures like `retrieval-signal-gap` are considered "true walls" with no designated seam.

#### 4.2. Loop Control Flow and Gating

The improvement loop operates in four phases per iteration, managed by an overarching control flow:
1.  **Diagnose:** The agent is run on an `OPTIMIZE` split (a development set). Failures are classified into regimes using fixed, oracle-based detectors. A histogram of failure regimes is constructed, and the dominant, optimizable, seam-reachable regime is selected for intervention.
2.  **Author:** A Large Language Model (LLM), specifically `claude-sonnet-4-6` in this study, drafts an executable transform corresponding to the selected regime's action seam. This is done based on failure signals from the `OPTIMIZE` split.
3.  **Gate:** Candidate transforms undergo a series of checks:
    *   **Static analysis:** Verifies against an import whitelist, checks signature, and enforces structural invariants (e.g., `reader-prompt-transform` can edit values but not fabricate keys, with a character limit).
    *   **Sandbox execution:** Runs the transform in an isolated environment.
    *   **In-sample evaluation (OPTIMIZE):** Evaluates the candidate on the `OPTIMIZE` split, requiring that the target regime's failures shrink and that overall performance does not regress.
4.  **Validate (Held-Out Gate):** If the candidate clears the in-sample gates, it is evaluated on a strictly held-out `CONFIRM` split. Promotion occurs only if the candidate's performance (`confirm_delta`) meets or exceeds a configurable `confirm_threshold` (defaulting to 0.0), indicating no regression on held-out data. This gate measures marginal improvement over the current deployed state.
    *   Upon promotion or exhaustion of attempts for a given regime, the loop rotates to the next seam-reachable regime, stopping when all are exhausted or a global iteration cap is reached. All outcomes (diagnoses, gate results, promotions, discards) are recorded as events in the system's log.

#### 4.3. Target-Agnostic Design

The loop's core logic is decoupled from specific agent tasks through a generic `Target` interface, which encapsulates the `EvalBackend`, `ActionSpace`, `RegimeTaxonomy`, and `outcome_summary` for any given task. This design was validated by extracting the interface from the LongMemEval-specific script under a "byte-identical loop behavior" constraint (ensuring the serialized event log remained identical before and after refactoring). A second target, a text-to-SQL agent, was then implemented against this interface to confirm interface and control-flow generality, though not empirical improvement across tasks.

#### 4.4. Experimental Setup

The experiments were conducted on the LongMemEval-S benchmark, comprising 500 questions. This set was stratified into an `OPTIMIZE` split of 50 questions (for diagnosis and in-sample evaluation) and a `CONFIRM` split of 100 held-out questions (for validation). Five fresh stratified splits (seeds 7, 11, 23, 5, 101) were used for replication, with `OPTIMIZE` and `CONFIRM` being disjoint within each run. The reader and author LLM was `claude-sonnet-4-6` (reader at temperature 0.0, author at 0.2). OpenAI `text-embedding-3-small` was used for embeddings.

#### 4.5. Failure Regime Classification and Determinism

The **regime classifier** is a deterministic oracle that uses gold evidence locations from the benchmark, rather than an LLM judge or heuristic. It assigns regimes based on predicate thresholds (e.g., `WELL_RANKED_K`, `ASSEMBLE_COVERAGE_FLOOR`). This ensures the diagnostic layer is independent of LLM nondeterminism and serves as an offline measurement instrument.

The underlying **ActiveGraph runtime** guarantees byte-reproducible replay of a recorded run from its event log. This includes caching model and tool responses, ensuring that replay does not involve new, potentially nondeterministic, model calls for the agent's actions. The paper clarifies that this guarantee applies to the non-LLM pipeline and cached responses, not to deterministic regeneration of LLM outputs from fresh API calls due to inherent hosted-model nondeterminism. The LongMemEval correctness judge (gpt-4o-2024-08-06) is external to the Regimes loop and its specific determinism claims, but is pinned to a dated snapshot to mitigate drift.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Regimes focused on its application to the LongMemEval-S benchmark, with a strong emphasis on held-out validation and reproducibility.

#### 5.1. Overall Held-Out Performance

The loop demonstrated a modest but directionally consistent held-out improvement on LongMemEval-S. Across five fresh stratified splits, four achieved positive held-out accuracy gains ranging from +0.05 to +0.10. The fifth split (seed 101) showed a +0.01 gain, which was attributed to over-promotion rather than a lack of initial effect.

#### 5.2. Multi-Seed Replication

Two of the five splits exhibited individual statistical significance under a simple paired McNemar test (seed 5: +0.10, p=0.006; seed 7: +0.08, p=0.039). Applying a Bonferroni correction for five tests, only seed 5 remained significant. As a descriptive pooled summary across all five splits, there were 43 wrong-to-right flips versus 13 right-to-wrong flips, resulting in a McNemar exact two-sided p-value of 7.3e-05. The paper emphasizes that this pooled value is descriptive and carries a "same-pool" caveat, as the splits are drawn from the same 500-question pool, meaning individual per-seed rows are considered the primary evidence.

The analysis of candidate funnels showed that the majority of discards occurred at the in-sample `OPTIMIZE` gate, with only two candidates rejected at the held-out `CONFIRM` gate across all runs. This indicates low selection pressure at the held-out validation stage, which helps bound multiplicity concerns.

#### 5.3. Analysis of Flips and Abstention

For seed 7, the most thoroughly analyzed split, the loop resulted in 10 wrong-to-right flips and 2 right-to-wrong flips on the held-out set, indicating a net improvement. Gains primarily localized to `multi-session` questions, with other question types showing minimal or no change.
Regarding abstention, no observed cost was reported: on the 6 held-out abstention questions, baseline and transformed accuracy remained at 6 correct. This suggests the anti-hedging instructions in the prompt repairs did not lead to an increase in false positives when evidence was genuinely absent.

Localization by regime for seed 7 showed that `assemble-internal` (the target of reader-prompt transforms) accounted for +8 net flips, while `budget-truncation` (retrieval-adjacent) showed +2 net flips, suggesting some spillover or oracle-label noise. The two regressions occurred on questions that were initially correct.

#### 5.4. Over-promotion and Stopping Rules

The seed 101 run served as a case study for over-promotion. On a high-baseline split, the loop promoted five `reader-prompt` transforms in sequence. While early promotes showed real gains (peaking at +0.09), later promotes were near-noise, leading the final state to only +0.01. This highlighted a limitation of the default `confirm_threshold = 0.0` and the absence of a plateau-aware stopping rule, allowing accumulation of prompt text without significant cumulative accuracy gains. The paper proposes a fix involving a stricter `confirm_threshold` (e.g., +0.02) and a plateau-detection stopping criterion.

#### 5.5. Mechanism Analysis: Regime-to-Seam Mapping

The `reader-prompt` seam, targeting `assemble-internal` failures, consistently contributed to positive held-out deltas across all five splits. The `score-transform` seam, targeting `budget-truncation`, was promoted only once, in seed 101, where `budget-truncation` was co-dominant (3 of 6 failures in the `OPTIMIZE` set). In other splits, `score-transforms` were largely discarded, often due to significant regressions in `multi-session` accuracy. This pattern is consistent with the `regime-to-seam` mapping, implying that effective repairs are localized to the appropriate pipeline stage for the dominant failure. However, the paper acknowledges that the direct marginal contribution of the routing step itself, compared to simply providing failed examples to an author with a held-out gate, remains an open question.

#### 5.6. Insights from Regressions

The two right-to-wrong flips observed in seed 7 provided insights into the limitations of prose-based prompt repairs. These regressions occurred because type-conditional prose rules within the promoted transform fired inappropriately. For example, a temporal rule designed to calculate age triggered on a temporal-retrieval question that required no arithmetic, and a supersession rule for `knowledge-update` questions misfired on a counting question (labeled `knowledge-update`) that required summation. This indicated that coarse type labels are lossy proxies for the actual reasoning needed, informing the thesis of "guarded operators."

#### 5.7. Generalization and Robustness

The generalization test, involving the successful re-homing of the loop onto a text-to-SQL target with byte-identical control flow, demonstrated the architectural generality of Regimes. Although empirical improvement was not claimed for the SQL target due to ceiling effects, it validated the target-agnostic design. A preliminary cross-author check using `claude-haiku-4-5` as the author for the `claude-sonnet-4-6` reader replicated gains on three significant splits, suggesting the improvements are not solely an artifact of shared author-reader identity.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The research offers several contributions with potential implications for the development and understanding of self-improving autonomous agents:

1.  **Auditable and Transparent Improvement Loops:** The core significance lies in demonstrating ActiveGraph as an auditable substrate that makes controlled agent improvement loops tractable. By logging every diagnosis, gate outcome, and promotion/discard decision as an event, the system enhances transparency and trustworthiness in agent self-modification, addressing a key challenge in autonomous system development. The guarantee of deterministic replay from the event log, combined with cached model responses, provides a foundation for inspecting and validating agent evolution.
2.  **Reproducible, Held-Out-Gated Improvement:** Regimes provides a concrete, empirically demonstrated instance of a reproducible improvement loop that rigorously employs held-out validation. This binding held-out gate is critical for distinguishing genuine, generalizable improvements from mere overfitting, a finding corroborated by concurrent work (GRASP). The study offers specific evidence of this gate rejecting overfit candidates, particularly with wider action spaces like free-text prompt edits.
3.  **Failure Regime Taxonomy as an Organizing Principle:** The introduction of an executable failure-regime taxonomy, which routes diagnosed failures to specific pipeline locations (action seams), provides a structured approach to agent debugging and repair. While the marginal value of this routing over an unrouted approach remains an open question, it serves as a design pattern for systematically addressing agent weaknesses.
4.  **Prompt as a Discovery Probe for Guarded Operators:** A significant forward-looking contribution is the hypothesis that prompt repairs, while yielding modest immediate gains, primarily function as discovery probes. Their successes highlight effective evidence-use behaviors, and their regressions pinpoint the precise structural conditions under which these behaviors should (or should not) fire. This thesis proposes a shift from optimizing prose prompts to distilling these discovered principles into durable, guarded deterministic operators that condition on detected structure rather than coarse linguistic cues. This reorients the goal of agent improvement towards more robust and interpretable internal mechanisms.
5.  **Practical Insights and Future Directions:** The paper identifies concrete limitations, such as the over-promotion phenomenon observed in seed 101, which directly motivates the development of stricter promotion thresholds and plateau-aware stopping rules. The analysis of regressions provides empirical grounding for the proposed "guarded operators" and outlines specific design sketches for such operators (e.g., `count_entities_across_sessions`, `select_current_fact_under_supersession`). The demonstration of the loop's target-agnostic interface also highlights its potential for broader applicability across different agent tasks.

In summary, this research positions the underlying system architecture as a crucial enabler for trustworthy autonomous improvement. While the empirical gains on the specific benchmark are modest, the durable contributions reside in the auditable substrate, the rigorous validation loop, and the conceptual framework for translating prompt-based discoveries into robust, interpretable agent capabilities.
