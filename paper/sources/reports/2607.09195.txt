The following report details the research paper "Toward Auditable AI Scientists: A Hypothesis Evolution Protocol for LLM Agents."

### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The research was conducted by Izumi Takahara and Teruyasu Mizoguchi, both affiliated with the Institute of Industrial Science at The University of Tokyo, Tokyo, Japan.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs) and LLM-based agents have positioned them as potential catalysts for scientific discovery across various domains. These agents leverage broad scientific knowledge, flexible reasoning capabilities, and tool-use functionalities to autonomously explore and address scientific problems. Existing applications include the automation of artificial intelligence research, biomedical discovery, and chemistry experiments, often by coupling LLMs with specialized domain tools and robotic laboratories. In materials science, LLM agents have been applied to computational materials design and the automation of synthesis and characterization, signaling a pathway to accelerate research progress.

However, the term "autonomous research" by LLM agents encompasses tasks with varying levels of agency. Much of the current work involves automating predefined workflows, executing prescribed sequences of operations, or optimizing towards externally specified goals. A higher level of agency, crucial for true scientific discovery, involves generating explanatory understanding through iterative cycles of mechanistic hypothesis generation, devising empirical tests, and revising beliefs based on the resulting evidence. While some efforts have moved in this direction by enabling agents to generate and rank hypotheses, or by integrating falsification into the research process, a fundamental limitation persists.

In current LLM agents, the core components of scientific reasoning—hypotheses, tests, and belief updates—are often embedded within unstructured logs or undisclosed internal states. This lack of explicit representation makes it challenging for both the agent and human researchers to audit the scientific process. Explicit beliefs are rarely tracked in a way that allows empirical evidence to systematically raise or lower their certainty. A recent analysis indicated that evidence is frequently ignored, and beliefs are inconsistently revised, even when refuted. Without a transparent and auditable record of the hypothesis–test–evidence–belief cycle, the scientific rigor of an agent's conclusions cannot be readily verified. Furthermore, the absence of such a structured framework hinders an agent's ability to systematically build upon, revise, or retire its own hypotheses over extended research periods. This work addresses these limitations by introducing a structured protocol to make the scientific reasoning process explicit and auditable.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to propose and evaluate the Hypothesis Evolution Protocol (HEP), an agent harness designed to transform the scientific procedure of proposing, testing, and revising hypotheses into explicit, auditable operations for LLM agents.

The motivation for developing HEP stems from several identified shortcomings in current LLM-based scientific agents:

*   **Lack of Auditability and Transparency:** In existing LLM agents, the process of scientific reasoning, including the generation of hypotheses, the design and execution of tests, and the subsequent updates to beliefs, is largely opaque. This information is typically buried within unstructured free-text logs or resides in internal, inaccessible states of the agent. This opaqueness prevents both the agent itself and human researchers from tracing the lineage of ideas, understanding how conclusions were reached, or identifying the specific evidence that influenced belief changes.
*   **Implicit Hypotheses and Beliefs:** Hypotheses and the agent's associated degrees of belief are often implicit rather than explicit, making it difficult to assess their validity or track their evolution. Without explicit externalization, it is challenging to verify the scientific soundness of an agent's reasoning.
*   **Inconsistent Evidence Integration:** Research has indicated that current LLM agents often fail to consistently incorporate empirical evidence into their belief structures. Evidence may be overlooked, and beliefs are not reliably updated even when confronted with refuting data. This undermines the iterative nature of scientific discovery, where hypotheses should be continuously refined or discarded based on new evidence.
*   **Challenges in Verification and Trust:** The absence of an explicit, auditable record of the hypothesis–test–evidence–belief cycle compromises the verifiability of an agent's scientific conclusions. For AI agents to become trustworthy partners in scientific discovery, their reasoning processes must be inspectable and verifiable by human researchers. This is crucial for establishing confidence in their findings and for building cumulative scientific knowledge.
*   **Hindrance to Long-Term Research and Evolution:** Without a structured mechanism to manage and evolve hypotheses over time, agents are limited in their capacity for sustained, open-ended scientific inquiry. They cannot systematically refine, merge, or retire hypotheses, which is essential for complex, multi-stage research problems.

In response to these motivations, HEP aims to externalize each hypothesis as a persistent, auditable object, explicitly linking its belief probability to attached evidence and tracking its progression through a defined lifecycle. This protocol is intended to serve as a structured framework that guides the agent through the scientific cycle in a disciplined manner. The authors sought to demonstrate that a HEP-equipped agent would actively engage in the hypothesis–test–evidence–belief cycle, generalize its application across diverse research questions, and exhibit enhanced protocol utilization with more capable underlying LLMs.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the Hypothesis Evolution Protocol (HEP), an agent harness designed to introduce explicit, auditable operations for hypothesis generation, evaluation, and evolution within LLM agents.

**Hypothesis Evolution Protocol (HEP) Components:**

1.  **HEP Registry:** This is an auditable, append-only, event-sourced data store that manages the state of every hypothesis.
    *   Each hypothesis is stored as a persistent object, uniquely identified, and includes a natural-language statement, a belief probability P(H) (representing the agent's current confidence in the hypothesis's truth), a lifecycle state, and its provenance.
    *   All operations, including belief updates, evidence attachments, and state transitions, are recorded as events appended to the hypothesis's event log. This ensures a complete, immutable audit trail.
    *   **Lifecycle States:** Hypotheses progress through five states: `proposed`, `under_test`, `supported`, `refuted`, or `dormant`.
    *   **Belief Discipline:** P(H) can only be altered by validated evidence. Verdicts (transition to `supported` or `refuted`) are enforced by thresholds: P(H) ≥ 0.8 for `supported` and P(H) ≤ 0.2 for `refuted`. Hypotheses that cannot be further tested are shelved as `dormant`.

2.  **HEP Tools:** These tools mediate the agent's interaction with the HEP Registry, categorized into three groups:
    *   **Propose & Evolve:**
        *   `propose_hypothesis`: Registers a new hypothesis.
        *   `refine_hypothesis`: Creates a child hypothesis based on an existing parent, which retains its own state.
        *   `merge_hypotheses`: Combines multiple hypotheses on the same question, transitioning unresolved parent hypotheses to `dormant` if subsumed.
        *   Hypotheses can be generated `de-novo`, `inspired-by` (tracking provenance without direct lineage), `refine`, or `merge`. Evolution mechanisms (`refine` and `merge`) are only permitted for parent hypotheses that have already reached a verdict or have at least one piece of attached evidence, preventing untested proposals from spawning descendants.
    *   **Test & Judge:**
        *   `attach_evidence`: Records a piece of evidence (e.g., simulation, experiment, literature) on a hypothesis and updates its belief. Evidence attachments require agent certification of trustworthiness for belief updates; uncertified attachments are recorded but do not change belief.
        *   `transition_hypothesis`: Moves a hypothesis between lifecycle states, with verdict transitions explicitly validated against belief thresholds.
    *   **Read:**
        *   `list_hypotheses`: Returns competing hypotheses grouped by research question, with current state and belief.
        *   `get_hypothesis`: Retrieves the full record of a single hypothesis, including its event timeline.

**Experimental Setup:**

*   **Agent Architecture:** A single autonomous research agent, implemented using the OpenAI Agents SDK, was used. It operated without human intervention, given a single task prompt.
*   **Tools Integration:** The agent was equipped with the HEP tools as well as computational tools for atomistic materials research (e.g., MLIP-based structure relaxation, molecular dynamics, materials-database queries from AtomisticSkills), an analysis code authoring/execution tool, and logging/reporting tools.
*   **Base LLM:** Experiments primarily used GPT-5.5. To assess the impact of LLM capability, comparative runs were conducted using GPT-5.4-mini and GPT-4.1.
*   **Run Settings:** Each iteration allowed up to 80 LLM turns before re-prompting. Conversation history was persisted. Runs had a 24-hour wall-clock budget.
*   **Stopping Condition:** A run concluded when the investigation saturated (no tool calls for three consecutive iterations), after which the agent was prompted to generate a final report. The HEP-equipped agent received a specific "hypothesis-discipline prompt."
*   **Baseline Comparison:** A planning-style agent (plan–execute–replan approach) with the same research goal, computational tools, and base LLM was used for comparison, but without the HEP harness or discipline prompt.

**Evaluation Tasks:**

HEP was evaluated on three materials-science research tasks, each involving an open research question:
1.  **Task AO2:** Determining the preferred crystal structure among seven polymorphs for compounds in the AO2 dioxides family and explaining the underlying physics/chemistry.
2.  **Task A2BB'O6:** Investigating B-site cation ordering (rock-salt, layered, columnar, disordered) in A2BB'O6 double perovskites.
3.  **Task MX:** Selecting the preferred prototype (rocksalt, NiAs, zincblende, wurtzite) in MX transition-metal monochalcogenides and monopnictides.
These tasks required explanatory understanding rather than optimization, and the behavior of the fixed machine-learned interatomic potential (MLIP) used for testing was unknown to the LLM beforehand.

**Trajectory Analysis:**

For the comparison between HEP and planning-style agents, each step of a run was categorized into loop elements: Hypothesis proposal (H), Test (T), Evidence (E), Belief update (B), Judgement (J), or Hypothesis update (U). HEP tool calls were deterministically mapped, computational tools to T, and free-text steps classified by an LLM. Consecutive repetitions of the same element were compressed, and row-normalized transition probabilities between adjacent elements were calculated and averaged across runs.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The study yielded several key findings regarding the Hypothesis Evolution Protocol (HEP) and its impact on LLM agent behavior.

**HEP Operates the Hypothesis–Test–Evidence–Belief Cycle:**
An example run on Task AO2 demonstrated the HEP-equipped agent's adherence to the full scientific cycle. The agent enumerated 16 candidate hypotheses, employing all four generation mechanisms (`de-novo`, `inspired-by`, `refine`, `merge`). All hypotheses reached a terminal lifecycle state (supported, refuted, or dormant). Notably, hypotheses converged through merging towards the end of the run, resulting in a final governing rule with a high belief probability (0.97). The generated hypothesis tree provided a clear, auditable trace of hypothesis lineage and evolution.

Analysis of belief trajectories for all 16 hypotheses showed that every belief change was directly linked to a recorded piece of validated evidence. The trajectories illustrated how evidence could raise a hypothesis's belief above the support threshold, drive it below the refutation threshold, or result in it being shelved as dormant if superseded or untestable. Verdicts were consistently grounded in evidence-driven belief changes.

A comparison with a planning-style agent (without HEP) highlighted the protocol's impact (Figure 2c). The planning-style agent spent 83% of its steps on tests and 0% on explicit belief updates, largely oscillating between hypotheses, tests, and evidence without formalizing belief revision. In contrast, the HEP-equipped agent exhibited clear transitions along the scientific loop: H→T (0.85), T→E (0.58), E→B (0.78), and B→J (0.40), with judgments further triggering hypothesis updates (J→U 0.22) that fed back into testing (U→T 0.65). This demonstrates that HEP explicitly drives the agent through a disciplined hypothesis–test–evidence–belief cycle, providing an auditable record that is absent in unstructured planning-style agent logs.

**HEP Generalizes Across Research Questions:**
HEP's effectiveness was consistent across the three distinct materials-science research tasks (AO2, A2BB'O6, MX). In all tasks, the agent generated 10–20 hypotheses, reaching generation depths of 3–5, and utilized all four hypothesis generation mechanisms. Furthermore, every hypothesis across all three tasks reached a terminal disposition (supported, refuted, or dormant), with `supported` being the most common outcome.

An examination of belief changes from proposal to verdict (Figure 3d) indicated that initial prior beliefs of hypotheses (irrespective of their eventual outcome) largely overlapped. However, final beliefs separated distinctly by the terminal state, suggesting that verdicts were primarily determined by the accumulated evidence rather than initial guesses. Belief flips, where the final belief crossed the 0.5 threshold from its prior, occurred in both directions and across all tasks. Interestingly, no hypothesis initially proposed with a prior belief above 0.7 was subsequently refuted; all such hypotheses ended up supported, which could suggest either accurate initial high priors or insufficient testing of highly believed hypotheses.

Analysis of prior beliefs assigned by the agent for different generation mechanisms (Figure 3e) revealed a monotonic increase: `de-novo` (mean 0.35), `inspired-by` (0.56), `refine` (0.70), and `merge` (0.81). This internal consistency suggests that hypotheses built upon more validated or established knowledge (e.g., refined or merged hypotheses) were systematically assigned higher priors.

Each task culminated in a supported governing rule (Table 1), which was verified by its predictive power on compositions not yet examined during the rule's formation. The historical records of these hypotheses, explicitly preserved in the HEP Registry, illustrated how belief flips influenced the research direction. For example, a hypothesis on Task A2BB'O6, initially proposed with skepticism (0.45), was driven to support (0.83) by evidence, becoming a cornerstone of the final rule. Conversely, plausible but incorrect hypotheses were explicitly refuted, redirecting the agent's search.

**HEP Scales with Base-LLM Capability:**
The study investigated how HEP utilization varied with the capability of the underlying LLM (GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4-mini, GPT-4.1) on Task AO2. As LLM capability decreased:
*   The mean number of generated hypotheses dropped from 14.7 (GPT-5.5) to 4.0 (GPT-4.1).
*   The mean maximum generation depth significantly decreased from 4.7 to 0.7. Weaker LLMs were less capable of growing hypotheses through refinement and merging.
*   The composition of generation mechanisms shifted; GPT-5.5 used a balanced repertoire, while weaker LLMs increasingly defaulted to `de-novo` proposals (from 25% for GPT-5.5 to 62% for GPT-4.1).
*   The proportion of unresolved hypotheses at the end of runs increased. GPT-5.5 resolved all hypotheses, whereas GPT-5.4-mini left 36% unresolved on average, and GPT-4.1 exhibited instability, with one run abandoning all proposed hypotheses without evidence attachment.

These results indicate that while weaker LLMs can operate individual HEP tools, a more capable LLM is required to fully command the protocol, exercising its complete set of tools and consistently evolving hypotheses to a definitive verdict. The value of HEP is therefore realized most effectively with highly capable base LLMs.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces the Hypothesis Evolution Protocol (HEP), a structured agent harness that transforms the implicit scientific reasoning processes of large language model (LLM) agents into explicit, auditable operations. This represents a significant step towards developing "auditable AI scientists."

The core significance lies in addressing the critical limitation of transparency and verifiability in current LLM-driven scientific discovery. By externalizing hypotheses as persistent, auditable objects with beliefs directly linked to validated evidence and evolving through a defined lifecycle, HEP enhances the scientific rigor and trustworthiness of AI agents. This explicit framework enables human researchers to inspect, verify, and build upon the agent's scientific reasoning, which was previously challenging due to unstructured logs and opaque internal states.

The findings demonstrate that HEP successfully guides LLM agents through the full hypothesis–test–evidence–belief cycle, a capability notably absent in planning-style agents. Its generalization across diverse materials-science research questions, with consistent operation of hypothesis generation, evolution, and resolution, indicates its broad applicability in scientific domains. Furthermore, the observation that the protocol's full exploitation scales with the base LLM's capability reinforces the complementary roles of agent harnesses and underlying model intelligence: while the LLM provides the raw reasoning power, the harness ensures that this reasoning is structured and verifiable.

**Potential Impact:**

*   **Enhanced Trust in AI-driven Science:** By providing an auditable trail of scientific reasoning, HEP can increase human confidence in AI-generated discoveries and conclusions, fostering greater collaboration between human and AI scientists.
*   **Improved Scientific Reproducibility and Verification:** The explicit record of hypotheses, evidence, and belief updates facilitates the reproduction and independent verification of AI agent research, aligning AI-driven discovery with established scientific practices.
*   **Facilitating Complex, Long-Term Research:** With a structured mechanism for hypothesis evolution, agents can undertake more complex, multi-stage research initiatives, systematically refining and building upon prior knowledge over extended periods.
*   **Foundational for Advanced AI Scientists:** HEP lays a foundation for future AI scientists that can engage in more sophisticated scientific inquiry, where reasoning can be inspected, challenged, and improved.

**Future Directions:**

The authors identify several promising avenues for future research:
*   **Rigor of Evidence Validation:** Moving beyond the agent's self-assessment of evidence validity, future work could incorporate programmatic checks for evidence where feasible, or introduce independent auditor agents to review evidence attachments and belief updates. The explicit nature of the HEP Registry makes such external validation directly actionable.
*   **Integration with First-Principles Tools:** Equipping agents with more accurate first-principles computational tools, such as density functional theory (DFT), could provide higher-fidelity evidence, further strengthening the scientific basis of belief updates.
*   **Multi-Agent Systems:** Extending HEP to multi-agent settings, where different agents could specialize in hypothesis generation, testing, or auditing, could lead to more robust and collaborative scientific discovery workflows.

In conclusion, this work marks a significant step toward creating auditable AI scientists whose scientific reasoning processes are transparent, verifiable, and capable of being systematically built upon by both AI and human researchers.
