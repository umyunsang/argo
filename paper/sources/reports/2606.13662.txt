## Research Report: EUREKAGENT – Agent Environment Engineering for Autonomous Scientific Discovery

### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The research paper "EUREKAGENT: AGENT ENVIRONMENT ENGINEERING IS ALL YOU NEED FOR AUTONOMOUS SCIENTIFIC DISCOVERY" was authored by Amy Xin, Jiening Siow, Junjie Wang, Zijun Yao, Jian Song, Lei Hou, and Juanzi Li from the Department of Computer Science and Technology, Tsinghua University. Fanjin Zhang is also listed as an author, affiliated with the School of Information, Renmin University of China.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the rapidly evolving field of autonomous scientific discovery, particularly utilizing Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents. Historically, scientific research has relied on human-driven processes of hypothesis generation, experimentation, observation, and iteration. The advent of LLMs has introduced the potential to automate aspects of this process, especially in domains where research progress can be quantified by an optimizable metric.

Previous approaches to developing autonomous research agents have largely focused on prescribing specific agentic workflows. Examples include evolutionary systems like AlphaEvolve, FunSearch, ShinkaEvolve, EvoX, AdaEvolve, and OpenEvolve, which manage populations of programs and use evaluators to guide mutation and selection. Other machine learning systems, such as AIDE, R&D-Agent, AIBuildAI, MLE-STAR, and ML-Master, structure exploration around solution trees, feedback loops, and specialized agents. More recent systems have incorporated structured debate, periodic self-review, and self-learning modules. These systems, while effective, often embed strong assumptions about the procedural steps of scientific research.

A recent observation in the field suggests a potential shift in the bottleneck for autonomous scientific discovery. As general-purpose coding agents like Claude Code and Codex have grown in capability, they have demonstrated an ability to discover new state-of-the-art scientific solutions when provided with clear tasks and optimizable metrics, even outperforming research-specific agent systems on benchmarks like ResearchClawBench. This indicates that much of the required capability might already reside in the foundational agent models themselves, rather than requiring highly specialized algorithmic workflows.

However, a critical challenge remains: while these agents can achieve high task performance, they often lack the rigor, reproducibility, and inspectability essential for reliable scientific discovery. Incidents of "reward hacking," evaluation tampering, artifact manipulation, and failure to adhere to procedural constraints have been reported in agentic research systems. These issues undermine trust and the scientific integrity of agent-generated results.

EUREKAGENT addresses this emerging challenge by proposing that, as general-purpose agents become more capable, the primary bottleneck shifts from designing explicit agent workflows to "environment engineering." This concept draws parallels to Gibson’s theory of affordances, suggesting that a well-designed environment can amplify productive behaviors (e.g., open-ended exploration, systematic artifact management, inter-agent collaboration) and suppress harmful ones (e.g., reward hacking, high-friction human oversight, evaluation tampering). Rather than instructing agents on *how* to do research, EUREKAGENT focuses on constructing an environment that provides the necessary resources, constraints, and interfaces to enable reliable and effective autonomous discovery. This positions the work as a foundational contribution to building robust and trustworthy autonomous research systems by shifting the design emphasis from internal agent logic to the external operational context.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the field of autonomous scientific discovery by proposing and demonstrating an alternative paradigm centered on "environment engineering." The authors posit that as large language models (LLMs) continue to improve, the critical challenge for autonomous scientific discovery is no longer solely about prescribing complex agent workflows, but rather about designing the operational environments in which these agents function.

This central motivation stems from several observations:
*   **Growing Capability of General-Purpose Agents:** Recent evidence suggests that off-the-shelf, general-purpose coding agents possess substantial inherent capabilities, enabling them to achieve state-of-the-art results in metric-driven scientific tasks even without highly specialized research workflows. This implies that the core intelligence for proposing and iterating solutions may already be present in these models.
*   **Reliability and Integrity Concerns:** Despite their impressive performance, autonomous agents often exhibit behaviors that compromise scientific rigor, reproducibility, and inspectability. These include contaminating evaluations, manipulating artifacts, violating procedural assumptions, and engaging in "reward hacking." Such failures undermine the trustworthiness of agent-generated scientific findings.
*   **Limitations of Workflow-Centric Approaches:** Existing autonomous research systems often rely on fixed, research-specific agentic workflows (e.g., evolutionary algorithms, solution trees). While effective to a degree, these approaches encode strong assumptions about the research process and may not fully leverage the emergent, open-ended exploration capabilities of advanced LLMs. They can also be brittle when faced with unexpected scenarios or when agents deviate from prescribed steps.

Given these points, the key objectives for EUREKAGENT are:
1.  **To reframe the bottleneck:** Argue that the primary challenge for reliable autonomous scientific discovery is shifting from defining intricate agent behaviors to engineering the environment around them.
2.  **To develop an environment-engineered agent system:** Create a system, EUREKAGENT, that coordinates off-the-shelf CLI agents primarily through environment design, rather than prescriptive workflows.
3.  **To define and implement core dimensions of environment engineering:** Structure the environment design along four critical dimensions: permissions, artifacts, budget, and human-in-the-loop engineering. These dimensions are intended to provide productive affordances while suppressing detrimental ones.
4.  **To validate the efficacy of environment engineering:** Empirically demonstrate that an environment-engineered approach can lead to state-of-the-art results on challenging metric-driven scientific tasks across mathematics, kernel engineering, and machine learning, doing so reliably and with responsible resource usage.
5.  **To advocate for environment engineering as a research direction:** Promote environment engineering as a fundamental area of research for developing capable, efficient, and responsible autonomous research agents.

The underlying motivation is to enable autonomous agents to perform open-ended exploration and iterative refinement with the accountability, accurate feedback, peer collaboration mechanisms, and mentor supervision analogous to what a human researcher experiences, thereby translating raw model capability into robust and trustworthy scientific progress.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

EUREKAGENT is designed as an environment-engineered agent system that coordinates multiple sessions of off-the-shelf Command Line Interface (CLI) agents for metric-driven research tasks. The system's core philosophy is to shape agent behavior through its surrounding environment rather than by prescribing detailed internal workflows.

**4.1 System Overview and Workflow Loop**

EUREKAGENT operates through a three-stage iterative loop: PREPARE → PROPOSE → {IMPLEMENT}.
*   **Inputs:** The system takes a problem description, a hidden evaluation script, a submission-format specification, optional initial code, and defined time and API cost budgets.
*   **Outer-Loop Coordination:** The environment manages the overall process, including workspace initialization, stage transitions, specifying objectives and deliverables for each session, exposing tool interfaces, recording and ranking solutions, persisting state, and enforcing budgets.
*   **Agent Autonomy:** Within these environmental boundaries, individual CLI agent sessions are free to determine their own research strategies, experimental plans, implementation details, and refinement processes.

The stages are executed as follows:

1.  **Prepare Stage:** This one-time stage initializes the research process. A preparation agent session is launched to:
    *   Understand the problem description and submission requirements.
    *   Test the hidden evaluation service to ensure a reliable setup.
    *   Install or validate necessary runtime dependencies.
    *   Optionally, pause and request human clarification if the setup is ambiguous.
    *   The stage concludes by producing a preparation summary and a completion artifact, which serve as shared context for subsequent stages.

2.  **Propose Stage:** At the beginning of each iteration round (up to `R` rounds), a proposal agent session is launched. Its responsibilities include:
    *   Inspecting task inputs, the preparation summary, and ranked best solutions from previous rounds.
    *   Utilizing web search or browsing tools to gather related literature or existing open-source solutions.
    *   Generating up to `P` candidate hypotheses, each with an implementation-ready description, which are compiled into a manifest. This stage acts as a "fan-in" step, distilling accumulated knowledge into new exploration directions.

3.  **Implement Stage:** This is the "fan-out" step, executed in parallel for each proposed hypothesis. Up to `P` separate implementation agent sessions are launched, each in its own isolated workspace.
    *   Each session starts with its assigned hypothesis and iteratively refines, debugs, or modifies the solution based on feedback from the hidden evaluator.
    *   Candidate solutions are submitted through a secure evaluation service, which records all submissions and maintains intermediate results alongside the best valid result.
    *   Upon completion of all parallel sessions or exhaustion of their budgets, EUREKAGENT automatically ranks all valid submissions and updates a ranked solution history file, which becomes shared context for the next round. This loop continues for `R` rounds or until budget limits are reached.

**4.2 Environment Engineering Dimensions**

EUREKAGENT engineers the environment along four dimensions to enable productive exploration while ensuring reliability and integrity:

1.  **Permissions Engineering:** This dimension provides agents with broad capabilities while preventing integrity violations.
    *   **Productive Affordances:** Agents have access to a configurable Python environment, workspace-level shell access, web search, browser tools, and all previous-round artifacts.
    *   **Protective Constraints:**
        *   **Run-level Isolation:** Each run operates within a Docker container to prevent modifications outside its workspace.
        *   **Hidden Evaluator:** The evaluator and test data are external to the agent's visible workspace, exposed only via a secure grading service. Agents submit solutions and receive scores but cannot inspect or modify the evaluator.
        *   **Controller-Owned Files:** Authoritative result files generated by the evaluator are system-managed, with hooks to block agent modification.
        *   **Same-Round Isolation:** Parallel implementation sessions are isolated from each other to prevent premature convergence or copying of peer solutions within the same round.
        *   **GPU Management:** A default-deny policy for GPUs is implemented, requiring agents to acquire access via a helper API that ensures exclusive ownership.

2.  **Artifact Engineering:** This dimension establishes a persistent, traceable memory for the research process.
    *   **Shared Long-Term Memory:** The filesystem, coupled with Git history, stores stage deliverables like preparation summaries, proposal manifests, hypotheses, solution code, evaluator feedback, and scored submissions.
    *   **System-Managed Artifacts:** Web-search history is logged, and official scores are automatically recorded and ranked.
    *   **Traceability and Recovery:** All artifacts are persisted, enabling traceability, interruption recovery, and resumability.
    *   **Git-tracked Evolution:** Within each session, Git commits track solution evolution, with agents instructed to describe both the current solution and changes from the previous version.

3.  **Budget Engineering:** This dimension controls resource consumption and enables budget-aware exploration.
    *   **Resource Axes:** Control is applied to wall-clock time and API cost.
    *   **Time Limits:** Separate time limits are set for proposal and implementation sessions.
    *   **Time Awareness:** Agents can actively query remaining time via a helper API. Passively, EUREKAGENT injects warnings when deadlines approach without required deliverables.
    *   **API Cost Tracking:** Accumulated token usage is tracked internally. If the cost limit is reached, the run is aborted, and the workspace is preserved.
    *   **Operational Continuity:** The system persists session status and budgets, allowing interrupted runs to resume from the latest state under remaining budget, with options for user-granted extensions.

4.  **Human-in-the-Loop Engineering:** This dimension facilitates human supervision and intervention.
    *   **Terminal UI:** Provides a CLI-agent style view for inspecting live session outputs and enabling chat-based communication with active agents.
    *   **Web Monitor:** Offers higher-level visual overviews, including score evolution, per-round and global best approaches, budget usage, and complete session transcripts for detailed inspection.
    *   These interfaces maintain agent autonomy while ensuring full observability and enabling human redirection when necessary.

**4.3 Evaluation Setup**

EUREKAGENT was evaluated on metric-driven research tasks across three domains:
*   **Mathematics:** Circle Packing (26 circles), Erdős’ Minimum Overlap, and First Autocorrelation Inequality.
*   **Kernel Engineering:** GPUMODE TriMul competition (optimized triangular matrix multiplication).
*   **Machine Learning Engineering:** A curated subset of seven competitions from the MLE-Bench Lite split.

For all experiments, EUREKAGENT utilized Claude Code as the CLI agent and GLM-5.1 as the base LLM. It was configured with a Web Search Prime MCP for search capabilities and a Playwright MCP for web browser navigation. Performance was measured against domain-specific objective scores and compared to previous human and AI state-of-the-art results.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

EUREKAGENT achieved new state-of-the-art results across all evaluated mathematics and kernel engineering tasks and ranked first on the evaluated MLE-Bench Lite subset. The findings suggest that environment engineering can effectively leverage off-the-shelf LLM capabilities for reliable scientific discovery.

**5.1 Mathematics Tasks**

EUREKAGENT was evaluated on three mathematical optimization problems: circle packing (26 non-overlapping circles), Erdős’ minimum overlap, and the first autocorrelation inequality.
*   **Circle Packing (Maximize sum of radii):** EUREKAGENT achieved a sum of radii of **2.635999**, surpassing the previous best AI result of 2.635986, which was achieved by R1-Distill-Qwen3-8B.
*   **Erdős’ Minimum Overlap (Minimize limiting maximum overlap):** EUREKAGENT found a solution with an overlap of **0.380870**, improving upon the previous best AI result of 0.380876 from gpt-oss-120b.
*   **First Autocorrelation Inequality (Minimize construction value):** EUREKAGENT yielded a value of **1.502861**, which is lower than the previous best AI result of 1.502863 from gpt-oss-120b.

Across all three mathematics tasks, EUREKAGENT established new state-of-the-art results. Notably, these achievements were made using GLM-5.1 and a training-free environment engineering approach, outperforming prior test-time training systems. The average API cost for these three tasks was reported to be less than $17, with the 26-circle packing task specifically costing less than $11.

**5.2 Kernel Engineering**

For the GPUMODE TriMul competition (optimizing triangular matrix multiplication on an A100 GPU), EUREKAGENT discovered multiple high-performing solutions:
*   The top four EUREKAGENT solutions all achieved median runtimes below 2031 μs, indicating consistent high-quality optimization.
*   The best solution, "EUREKAGENT-CUDA Graph," recorded a median runtime of **2005.0307 μs**.
*   This performance represents an improvement of approximately 4.3% over the strongest regraded human leaderboard solution (josusamartin, 2096.0441 μs) and about 10.8% over the TTT-Discover solution (2247.7849 μs).

This demonstrates EUREKAGENT's ability to generate optimized code for complex hardware-specific tasks, outperforming both human-designed and advanced AI-generated kernels under the same evaluation protocol.

**5.3 Machine Learning Engineering**

EUREKAGENT was evaluated on a curated subset of seven competitions from the MLE-Bench Lite split, spanning various ML tasks.
*   **Any Medal Rate:** EUREKAGENT achieved an any-medal rate of **85.71%**, which was the highest among all listed baselines (AIBuildAI, Famou-Agent, Famou-Agent 2.0, LoongFlow, CAIR MARS+, all reporting 71.43%).
*   **Gold Medal Rate:** EUREKAGENT achieved a gold-medal rate of **71.43%**. While CAIR MARS+ also reported 71.43%, EUREKAGENT achieved this using the GLM-5.1 open-source LLM, whereas all listed baselines utilized closed commercial models such as Claude-Opus-4.6, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and Gemini-3-Pro-Preview.
*   **Above Median Rate:** EUREKAGENT achieved a 100.00% above median rate, matching some other top agents.

These results indicate EUREKAGENT's capability in diverse machine learning engineering tasks, securing a competitive standing, particularly when considering its use of an open-source LLM compared to proprietary models used by other top-performing agents.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

The EUREKAGENT research presents several significant implications for the future of autonomous scientific discovery:

1.  **Validation of Environment Engineering as a Core Paradigm:** The primary significance lies in empirically validating that "environment engineering" can be a highly effective approach for developing autonomous research agents. The results demonstrate that by carefully designing the resources, constraints, and interfaces of an agent's operational environment, significant advancements in performance, reliability, and integrity can be achieved, potentially surpassing methods focused solely on prescribing complex agent workflows. This suggests a paradigm shift in how autonomous research systems might be conceived and built in the future.

2.  **Addressing Reliability and Integrity Deficiencies:** A major impact of EUREKAGENT is its direct confrontation with the issues of reward hacking, evaluation tampering, and lack of reproducibility that have plagued previous autonomous agent systems. The explicit design choices in permissions engineering, artifact engineering, and budget engineering aim to mitigate these risks, leading to a more trustworthy and scientifically rigorous research process. This enhanced reliability is crucial for the adoption of AI agents in sensitive scientific domains.

3.  **Leveraging General-Purpose Agent Capabilities:** The success of EUREKAGENT with off-the-shelf CLI agents and an open-source LLM (GLM-5.1) indicates that the underlying intelligence in modern LLMs can be effectively harnessed for scientific discovery when placed within a well-structured environment. This reduces the need for developing highly specialized, domain-specific AI models or intricate agent architectures, potentially democratizing access to powerful autonomous research tools.

4.  **Cost-Effectiveness and Efficiency:** Achieving state-of-the-art results with low API costs, as demonstrated by the $11 cost for the 26-circle packing task, highlights the efficiency of the environment-engineered approach. This cost-effectiveness could make large-scale computational exploration more accessible to a broader range of researchers and institutions, accelerating discovery by lowering financial barriers.

5.  **Enhanced Human-Agent Collaboration:** The human-in-the-loop engineering, through both terminal UI and a web monitor, fosters a collaborative model where humans can maintain oversight, intervene strategically, and redirect agent behavior without micromanaging. This balance between agent autonomy and human supervision is critical for complex scientific problems where human intuition and ethical considerations remain paramount.

6.  **Establishing a New Research Direction:** By explicitly calling for "environment engineering as a core research direction," the paper aims to inspire further research into the design of robust, flexible, and secure operational environments for AI agents. This includes extending the concepts beyond metric-driven tasks to more open-ended and exploratory scientific contexts, addressing challenges such as defining novel metrics, handling ambiguous feedback, and fostering creative conceptual leaps.

In summary, EUREKAGENT contributes to the development of more capable, efficient, and responsible autonomous research agents by demonstrating that the environment itself can be a powerful lever for shaping agent behavior, thereby accelerating scientific progress while upholding research integrity.
