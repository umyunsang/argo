### Research Report: EVO-BENCH: CAN LANGUAGE MODELS IMPROVE AGENT HARNESS?

**1. Authors and Institution(s)**

The research was conducted by:
*   Lisheng Huang, Huatong Song, and Wayne Xin Zhao from the Gaoling School of Artificial Intelligence, Renmin University of China.
*   Chen Yang, Hao Zhou, Zongchao Chen, Ran Le, and Yang Song from BOSS Zhipin, Beijing, China.

**2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape**

The field of artificial intelligence has experienced rapid advancements driven by Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly in their application to autonomous agent systems. These agents are designed to execute complex, long-horizon tasks, moving beyond static problem-solving to more dynamic, real-world interactions. The performance of such agents is not solely dependent on the underlying LLM's capabilities but also significantly influenced by "agent harnesses." These harnesses are the structured frameworks, code, and mechanisms that orchestrate, coordinate, and constrain agent behavior, effectively guiding how an LLM interacts with its environment and utilizes tools. Examples of sophisticated agent harnesses include those developed for models like Claude Code and Codex.

An emerging area of research focuses on self-improving agentic systems, where agents autonomously enhance their own capabilities. This self-improvement manifests in various forms, such as autonomous scientific research, automated model post-training, and, notably, autonomous harness evolution. Harness evolution is considered a foundational step towards achieving broader agentic self-improvement. While existing work has explored the methodologies of harness evolution, ranging from prompt searching to refactoring executable code scaffolds, and has evaluated these methods in specific contexts, a systematic and comprehensive benchmark for an LLM's intrinsic capacity for general-purpose harness evolution has been identified as a gap.

Current agent benchmarks primarily assess task execution within fixed harnesses, and AI R&D benchmarks typically evaluate optimization of model weights or training pipelines. Although some emerging evaluations track self-evolutionary dynamics or focus on task-specific artifact generation, they often do not explicitly benchmark foundation models as long-horizon research agents evolving a single, shared, general-purpose harness under rigorously controlled conditions. This paper introduces Evo-Bench to address this specific void, aiming to provide a standardized, robust evaluation framework for LLMs' ability to autonomously improve their operational harnesses across diverse domains.

**3. Key Objectives and Motivation**

The central question motivating this research is whether large language models can genuinely improve agent harnesses, and how this evolutionary capability can be systematically benchmarked. The authors identify three specific challenges that existing evaluation paradigms for harness evolution fail to adequately address:

*   **Harness Sensitivity:** Benchmark task performance must be genuinely responsive to improvements in the agent harness and not predominantly influenced by the inherent strength or limitations of the base LLM itself. If task performance is largely dictated by the LLM's core capabilities, it becomes difficult to isolate and measure the impact of harness modifications.
*   **Cross-Split Generalization:** The validation and evaluation task splits must exhibit aligned responsiveness to harness improvements. This is crucial to prevent the LLM from overfitting to the validation tasks, where improvements might be specific to those tasks and not generalize to unseen evaluation scenarios. Robust generalization across different but related task sets is a key indicator of effective harness evolution.
*   **Long-Horizon Evolution:** Evaluating true harness evolution requires assessing a model's capacity for sustained, multi-round iterative refinement. This involves the agent autonomously diagnosing failure patterns, formulating improvement hypotheses, implementing and testing those hypotheses through code updates, and progressively refining the harness over an extended period. Existing evaluations often fall short in capturing this iterative, long-term research process.

To address these challenges, the primary objective of this work is to introduce **Evo-Bench**. Evo-Bench is presented as the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate LLMs' intrinsic harness-evolving capabilities. It aims to achieve this by providing a controlled, long-horizon research setting where a fixed policy model is subjected to iterative harness improvements orchestrated by an "evolver" LLM. The benchmark spans diverse domains and incorporates a novel harness-guided construction framework to ensure tasks are sensitive to harness changes and that generalization across validation and evaluation suites is robustly measured.

**4. Methodology and Approach**

Evo-Bench is structured to evaluate an LLM's ability to conduct long-horizon, code-centric iterative improvement of an executable agent harness. The core setup involves a fixed policy model (π) and an evolver (E) that iteratively refines the policy harness (H).

**Task Formulation:**
At each evolution iteration *t*, the policy agent `A_task_t` operates with the policy model π and the current harness `H_t`. The evolver `A_evo` operates through a separate, fixed evolve harness `H_evo`. The evolver inspects cumulative validation-side evidence, edits `H_t`, and requests evaluation on a visible validation suite (`D_val`). The process is constrained by a fixed resource budget (iterations, wall-clock time, evolver steps). Upon termination, the final harness `H_T` is evaluated on a disjoint held-out evaluation suite (`D_eval`).

**Task Domains and Composition:**
Evo-Bench covers three agent domains:
*   **Search:** Tasks requiring information retrieval and web navigation (from BrowseComp, HLE).
*   **Office:** Tasks involving specific processing workflows and document manipulation (from GDPval, APEX-Agents).
*   **General Agent Tasks:** Broader, multi-step problem-solving scenarios (from Claw-Eval).
The benchmark comprises a 160-task validation suite and a 448-task evaluation suite, ensuring strict separation.

**Evolve Harness (`H_evo`):**
This fixed agent loop supports long-horizon autonomous work. Its components include:
*   **System Prompt:** Defines optimization objective, workflow, editable surfaces, and integrity rules.
*   **Execution Tools:** File inspection, editing, code search, shell execution, web access, and read-only delegated exploration.
*   **Context Manager:** Tracks budgets, evaluations, best revisions, and a persistent experiment ledger, summarizing old interaction history.
*   **Specialized Skills:** Trajectory analysis (e.g., `query_rollouts`, `diff_task`, `analyze_evals`), experiment tracking (`log_experiment`, `record_insight`), and architectural reflection (`architecture_checkpoint`).
*   **Evaluation Interface:** `run_train_eval` for asynchronous evaluation of immutable snapshots and `await_eval` for fetching results.
*   **Sandbox and Snapshot Management:** Separates evolver evidence from policy inputs, records revisions, and freezes a selected harness for held-out evaluation.

**Policy Harness (`H`):**
The evolving artifact, starting as a minimal `CodeAct` loop (`H_0`) with only a shell-execution tool and a final-answer completion tool. The evolver aims to transform `H_0` into a more capable, generalizable harness.

**Evaluation Metrics:**
*   **Overall Score (`Overall(E)`):** The aggregate score of `S(π, H_T; D_eval)` on the unseen evaluation suite, using native benchmark scorers.
*   **Anytime Validation Score (`AnytimeVal(E)`):** Average of the best-so-far validation performance `max_i≤t S(π, H_i; D_val)` over the evolution budget.

**Benchmark Construction Framework (Two-Stage Harness-Guided):**
This framework ensures tasks are sensitive to harness improvements and that validation/evaluation splits align.

**Stage 1: Auxiliary Harness Generation**
1.  **Auxiliary Tasks Collection:** Tasks are collected from diverse sources (e.g., MiroRL, RedSearcher, Auto-ClawEval) distinct from the main Evo-Bench source benchmarks. These tasks are evaluated with a high-performing LLM (DeepSeek-V4-Flash) using domain-native workflows. Tasks with lower average scores and longer interaction trajectories (indicating more room for improvement) are selected, forming a 320-task auxiliary set (128 search, 128 office, 64 general).
2.  **Auxiliary Harness Evolution:** Four frontier models (GLM-5.2, Claude-Opus-4.8, Claude-Sonnet-5, GPT-5.6-Sol) independently evolve the seed `CodeAct` harness on the auxiliary task set, using the fixed policy model and benchmark evaluation protocol. This generates 73 formally evaluated harness variants. A diversity-aware selection procedure (k-medoids) identifies 12 representative harnesses (`H_aux`) that maximize diversity across capabilities, tool orchestration, program structure, and auxiliary-task performance.

**Stage 2: Harness-Guided Task Selection**
1.  **Candidate Task Evaluation:** 2,329 candidate tasks from the five main source benchmarks are evaluated using the 12 selected auxiliary harnesses (`H_aux`). Multimodal tasks are excluded.
2.  **Harness Sensitivity and Task Difficulty Measurement:**
    *   **Harness Sensitivity (`Sens(x)`):** Computed as the Pearson correlation coefficient between a task's score under different harnesses `m_h(x)` and the overall quality of each harness `Q_(-x)_h` (average performance across other tasks). Higher sensitivity indicates that the task reliably reflects harness quality.
    *   **Task Difficulty (`1 - Perf(x)`):** `Perf(x)` is the average performance of task *x* across `H_aux`. Lower average performance indicates higher difficulty and more performance headroom for evolution.
3.  **Task Suite Construction:**
    *   Tasks with non-positive `Sens(x)` are filtered out.
    *   Remaining tasks are partitioned into difficulty strata based on `1 - Perf(x)`.
    *   Tasks with the highest `Sens(x)` within each stratum are selected to ensure difficulty diversity while prioritizing responsiveness to harness evolution.
    *   Finally, tasks are randomly split within each stratum into validation (`D_val`) and evaluation (`D_eval`) suites, ensuring consistent difficulty distributions across splits.

**Experimental Setup:**
*   **Policy Model:** DeepSeek-V4-Flash is consistently used as the fixed policy model for all main experiments.
*   **Evolver Models:** Seven frontier models and two open-weight models are evaluated (e.g., GPT-5.6-Sol, Claude Opus 4.8, GLM-5.2, Qwen3.7-Max).
*   **Budget:** Main experiments use 20 iterations, 1,000 evolver steps, and 48 hours. Policy rollouts are capped at 300 steps and one hour.
*   **Grading:** LLM-based grading for certain tasks uses Qwen3.7-Plus as a common judge.
*   **Integrity and Reward-Hacking Analysis:** Strict sandbox separation, scanning for benchmark-specific answer patterns, and semantic audits are employed to detect and mitigate policy violation or reward-hacking behaviors.

**5. Main Findings and Results**

The evaluation of nine frontier and open-weight models on Evo-Bench yielded several observations regarding their harness-evolving capabilities.

**Overall Performance:**
*   Top evolvers, GPT-5.6 Sol and Claude Opus 4.8, achieved substantial absolute performance gains of 16.6 and 16.1 points, respectively, over the initial CodeAct seed harness. This indicates that frontier LLMs possess a demonstrable capacity to autonomously optimize executable harnesses.
*   Despite these gains, the top evolved harness (Overall Score of 46.3 for GPT-5.6 Sol) still slightly underperformed a composite human-engineered "Artificial Harness" baseline (47.5), suggesting remaining headroom for further improvement.
*   In terms of evolutionary progress, Claude Opus 4.8 and GLM-5.2 achieved the highest Anytime Validation scores, suggesting rapid discovery of high-quality structures but a tendency to introduce detrimental modifications in later iterations.

**Per-Domain Performance:**
*   **Search Tasks:** This domain was most amenable to optimization. Claude Opus 4.8 achieved massive gains of +34.8 points, nearly matching the Artificial Harness baseline. This suggests evolvers effectively synthesize web-navigation logic.
*   **Office Tasks:** This domain proved more challenging. Most models showed only marginal improvements or slight performance regressions, failing to match the Artificial Harness baseline. This indicates difficulty in discovering highly specific and complex processing workflows.
*   **General Tasks:** Top evolvers, such as GPT-5.6-Sol and Qwen3.7-Max, surpassed the Artificial Harness baseline in this domain. This outcome suggests that autonomously evolved reasoning structures can, in certain contexts, outperform manually crafted solutions.

**Budget Usage:**
*   Two distinct evolutionary behaviors were observed:
    *   **Exhaustive Exploration:** GPT-5.6-Sol and Kimi-K2.7-Code uniquely utilized their maximum iteration, step, and time budgets, contributing to GPT-5.6-Sol's top performance.
    *   **Early Saturation/Premature Termination:** Most other models terminated prematurely due to invalid code proposals or stagnant reasoning loops. For instance, Qwen3.7-Max and DeepSeek-V4-Pro halted early despite unused budget.
*   Budget exhaustion did not strictly correlate with success. Qwen3.7-Max, despite early halting, achieved competitive scores, particularly excelling in General tasks, demonstrating efficiency in synthesizing effective updates.

**Cost Analysis:**
*   A steep logarithmic trade-off existed between financial investment (evolver API inference cost) and evolutionary capability.
*   Achieving top-tier results required substantial resources, with GPT-5.6-Sol exceeding 500 USD per run.
*   Models in the middle ground, like GLM-5.2 and Qwen3.7-Max, demonstrated strong cost-effectiveness, delivering competitive performance for under 40 USD.
*   DeepSeek-V4-Pro anchored the absolute baseline, synthesizing functional improvements for less than a dollar.

**Case Study: GPT-5.6-Sol's Harness Evolution:**
*   GPT-5.6-Sol demonstrated capabilities as an autonomous systems engineer, constructing a hierarchical router for domain-specific prompts and tools, tool construction (e.g., web-search/fetch/cleaner), failure diagnosis, and iterative correction.
*   For Search, it implemented webpage cleaning to remove noise. For Office, it differentiated APEX (evidence tracking) and GDPval (artifact creation/recalculation). For General tasks, it added recovery from empty responses and safety guards.

**Challenges and Limitations:**
*   **Superficial Analysis:** LLMs often reacted superficially to aggregate scores instead of distilling causal failure modes from detailed logs.
*   **Naive Routing:** Cross-domain interference was resolved via simple domain routing rather than discovering fundamentally robust, shared mechanisms.
*   **Underutilized Budget:** Models frequently underutilized the research budget, lacking the persistent, goal-oriented approach of human engineers.
*   **Localized Modifications:** The evolved harnesses primarily relied on localized modifications, leaving core components (planner, context, verifier) relatively primitive. These patterns were consistent across various evolvers.

**Ablation Studies:**
*   **Effect of Evolution Budget:** Increasing the budget (from 24h/10 iterations to 48h/20 iterations) resulted in consistent, monotonic improvements in both Overall Score and Anytime Validation Score for Qwen3.7-Max and GLM-5.2, indicating positive scaling of harness evolution with resources.
*   **Effect of Policy Model:** The evolved harnesses demonstrated robust generalization across different underlying policy models (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, DeepSeek-V4-Flash, GLM-5.2). Even with policy models starting at lower baselines, evolvers consistently achieved significant improvements, indicating they synthesized generalizable reasoning and tool-use structures rather than overfitting to a specific policy model's idiosyncrasies.

**6. Significance and Potential Impact**

Evo-Bench represents a significant contribution to the field of autonomous agents and LLM evaluation by establishing the first benchmark specifically designed to measure LLMs' intrinsic capability for harness evolution. This work addresses critical limitations of prior evaluation paradigms by providing a rigorous framework that allows for controlled attribution of performance gains to harness improvements, supports long-horizon iterative refinement, and ensures robust generalization across task splits.

The benchmark's design disentangles genuine harness-evolving capability from superficial prompt tuning or stochastic runtime noise, offering a clearer signal for research progress. The comprehensive evaluations across frontier LLMs provide a systematic account of their current abilities as autonomous research engineers. The findings demonstrate that LLMs possess a promising capacity to autonomously optimize executable harnesses, leading to substantial performance gains and, in some cases, even surpassing human-engineered solutions for general tasks.

The identified limitations, such as superficial failure analysis, naive architectural changes, underutilization of computational budgets, and a tendency towards localized modifications, offer concrete directions for future research. By highlighting these challenges, Evo-Bench can catalyze advancements in areas like causal failure abstraction, large-scale architectural refactoring by LLMs, and more strategic budget utilization.

Ultimately, Evo-Bench serves as a foundational testbed to foster the development of self-evolving agent architectures and more sophisticated execution-grounded reasoning. The insights gained from this benchmark are crucial for guiding the next generation of AI systems towards greater autonomy and self-improvement, potentially enabling LLMs to continuously evolve harnesses that consistently exceed human expert capabilities across diverse, complex domains. The robust transferability of evolved structures across distinct policy models underscores the potential for these autonomously designed components to become general-purpose enhancements for a wide range of agent systems.
