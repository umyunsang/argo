## Research Paper Report: Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution of Coding-Agent Harnesses

### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The research was conducted by a collaborative team from Fudan University (Jiahang Lin, Shichun Liu, Shihan Dou, Zhiheng Xi, Xuanjing Huang, Tao Gui, Yu-Gang Jiang), Peking University (Chengjun Pan), and Shanghai Qiji Zhifeng Co., Ltd (Lizhi Lin, Hang Yan, Zhenhua Han). Jiahang Lin, Shichun Liu, and Chengjun Pan contributed equally, and their work was performed during an internship at Shanghai Qiji Zhifeng Co., Ltd. Zhenhua Han and Tao Gui are noted as corresponding authors.

### 2. Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence is increasingly focused on the deployment of coding agents for complex, long-horizon software engineering tasks, which has seen measurable advancements in areas such as resolving issues in real-world code repositories and executing multi-step terminal workflows. The performance of these coding agents is not solely dependent on the underlying large language model (LLM) but also significantly on a collection of external, editable components referred to as the "harness." This harness includes elements like the system prompt, tools for interacting with the file system and shell, and middleware for context management, execution control, and recovery mechanisms.

Currently, the design and optimization of these harnesses predominantly rely on manual engineering. Developers iteratively inspect agent trajectories, identify recurring failure patterns, and manually craft adjustments across various components. However, this manual process struggles to keep pace with the rapid advancements and frequent updates in base language models. This disparity creates a gap where the full potential of advanced LLMs cannot be realized due to outdated or suboptimal harnesses.

Previous efforts to automate agent optimization have primarily focused on refining individual components, such as system prompts, in-context playbooks, or skill libraries. Few approaches have jointly evolved the entire set of editable harness components. End-to-end evolution of multiple components presents challenges, including the difficulty of extracting actionable signals from long, unstructured trajectories and the error-prone nature of edits in tightly coupled harness frameworks. This research positions itself by addressing the fundamental question of how an evolution agent can jointly and stably optimize all editable components of a coding agent’s harness. It postulates that the bottleneck is not necessarily agent capability but rather the lack of effective observability into the agent's actions, experiences, and decisions.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE), a closed-loop system designed to automate the evolution of coding-agent harnesses. This system aims to enable an evolution agent to jointly and stably optimize all editable components of a coding agent's harness, thereby overcoming the limitations of manual harness engineering and partial automation methods.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in current coding agent development:
1.  **Manual Harness Engineering Limitations:** The current practice of manually designing and adapting harnesses is time-consuming and labor-intensive. As base language models advance rapidly, this manual loop creates a widening gap, preventing coding agents from fully leveraging new model capabilities.
2.  **Model-Specific Optimal Harnesses:** The optimal harness configuration is often specific to a particular base model. When a base model is updated or swapped, the harness typically needs re-adaptation, perpetuating the manual effort.
3.  **Lack of Joint Component Evolution:** Most existing automated optimization approaches focus on single components (e.g., prompts or skills). Jointly evolving the full suite of harness components (system prompt, tools, middleware, skills, memory) is more complex but potentially more impactful, as these components interact and influence overall agent performance.
4.  **Challenges in Automated Evolution:** Automating the joint evolution of harnesses faces structural obstacles:
    *   **Heterogeneous Action Space:** Harness components are diverse, making it difficult to define a unified action space for automated editing.
    *   **Voluminous Trajectories:** Raw agent trajectories are typically long and unstructured, burying actionable signals needed for effective optimization.
    *   **Attribution of Edit Effects:** It is challenging to accurately attribute the impact of specific harness edits, especially when multiple components are involved or when effects are subtle or delayed.

The central insight motivating AHE is that these challenges are primarily bottlenecked by "observability." The authors hypothesize that if an evolution agent is provided with structured context over a clear action space, it can reliably converge on superior harness designs. Therefore, the key objective is to address this observability bottleneck through a novel design that explicitly structures the action space, distills actionable signals from experience, and enables transparent attribution of edit effects.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

AHE operates as a closed-loop system where an evolution agent optimizes harness components while the base language model remains fixed. The methodology is structured around three "observability pillars" designed to provide the evolution agent with the necessary structured context and action space.

#### 4.1. Component Observability: NexAU Harness Substrate

The first pillar, component observability, is realized through a decoupled, file-level harness substrate implemented using the NexAU framework. This framework exposes seven distinct component types as explicit files at fixed locations within a single workspace:
*   **System Prompt:** Shapes the agent's overall work style.
*   **Tool Description:** Defines the interface and usage of tools.
*   **Tool Implementation:** Contains the actual code for tools (e.g., file system, shell access).
*   **Middleware:** Controls context, execution, and recovery through hooks into the agent's loop.
*   **Skill:** Encapsulates reusable workflow patterns.
*   **Sub-Agent Configuration:** Defines specialized child agents for delegated tasks.
*   **Long-Term Memory:** Stores persistent cross-session knowledge.

The loose coupling of these component types ensures that a failure pattern can be cleanly mapped to a specific component class. This design provides the evolution agent with a clear and localized action space, where changes to one component do not necessitate widespread modifications in others. Each logical edit is recorded as a commit in the workspace's Git history, enabling file-level diffs and rollback capabilities. The initial harness, denoted NexAU0, is deliberately minimal, starting with only a basic shell-execution tool, to ensure that all subsequent performance gains are directly attributable to AHE's evolutionary process.

#### 4.2. Experience Observability: Agent Debugger

The second pillar, experience observability, transforms raw agent trajectories into a structured, actionable evidence corpus using the Agent Debugger framework. For each task in a benchmark, `k` traces are generated using the current harness. These raw traces, which can contain millions of tokens, are then processed to extract insights.
*   An agent-based debugger explores trajectories, treating each message as a file in a navigable environment accessible via generic shell tools.
*   Traces for the same query are grouped, and the debugger is tasked with analyzing the root cause of failures or identifying success patterns.
*   Per-task analysis reports are generated, summarizing findings and including task pass/fail status to ground the Evolve Agent's decisions.
*   A benchmark-level overview aggregates insights from all reports, serving as an entry point for each evolution iteration.
*   Original raw and lightly processed traces are also retained, allowing the Evolve Agent to verify claims in the reports if necessary. This progressive disclosure of information helps conserve tokens and supports better decision-making by the agent.

#### 4.3. Decision Observability: Evolve Agent and Change Manifest

The third pillar, decision observability, is realized through the Evolve Agent and a "change manifest." The Evolve Agent closes the AHE loop by performing the following actions in each round:
*   It reads the layered evidence corpus generated by the Agent Debugger.
*   It decides which harness components to add, modify, or remove.
*   It applies these edits to the workspace.
*   It records the reasoning behind every edit in a versioned manifest.

Two key constraints govern these edits, ensuring decision observability:
1.  **Controllability:** The Evolve Agent is restricted to writing only within the `workspace/` directory. Directories like `runs/`, the tracer, verifier, and LLM configuration are read-only. Crucially, original system prompt rules (from iteration 1) cannot be deleted. These restrictions prevent the agent from taking shortcuts (e.g., disabling the verifier or changing the base model) and ensure that all recorded gains are genuinely attributable to harness edits.
2.  **Evidence-Driven Changes with Predicted Impact:** Every change made by the Evolve Agent must be justified by concrete evidence. Each edit is associated with a manifest entry detailing:
    *   The specific failure evidence.
    *   The inferred root cause.
    *   The targeted fix.
    *   A predicted impact, including both expected fixes and tasks at risk of regression.

This manifest transforms each edit into a "falsifiable contract." In the subsequent round, the loop compares these predictions against observed task-level outcomes. Edits whose predictions are not verified can be reverted at file granularity, replacing subjective rationale with measurable performance deltas.

#### 4.4. AHE Outer Loop

Algorithm 1 orchestrates these three pillars into an iterative process:
1.  **Rollout:** The current harness is used to generate `k` rollouts for each benchmark task.
2.  **Clean:** Traces are normalized into a canonical form.
3.  **Attribute and Rollback (from iteration 2 onwards):** The previous round's manifest is attributed (verified), and rejected edits are rolled back.
4.  **Distill:** The Agent Debugger processes the cleaned traces to generate layered evidence.
5.  **Evolve:** The Evolve Agent uses the current harness, the distilled evidence, and the attribution verdict to make new edits and generate a new change manifest.
6.  **Commit:** The updated harness and manifest are committed to Git.
7.  **Update Best:** The overall best-performing harness is tracked.

An initial one-shot "explore agent" runs in parallel with iteration 1 to seed a small number of reusable skills from external sources, which the Evolve Agent can subsequently refine or remove.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The empirical validation of AHE addressed three research questions concerning its performance against baselines, its transferability, and the internal drivers of its gains.

#### 5.1. RQ1: Performance Against Baselines on Terminal-Bench 2

A single AHE campaign, consisting of ten iterations from the minimal NexAU0 seed on Terminal-Bench 2, resulted in a significant improvement in performance.
*   **Pass@1 Improvement:** AHE lifted the pass@1 score from 69.7% (NexAU0 seed) to 77.0%.
*   **Superiority Over Baselines:** This performance surpassed both human-designed harnesses (OpenCode 47.2%, Terminus-2 62.9%, Codex 71.9%) and other self-evolving baselines (ACE 68.9%, Training-Free GRPO 72.3%).
*   **Cumulative Gain:** The pass@1 score accumulated consistently across iterations, demonstrating continuous improvement.
*   **Difficulty Analysis:** AHE performed strongly across task difficulties, achieving 100% on Easy and 88.2% on Medium tasks. It marginally trailed Codex on Hard tasks (53.3% vs. 56.7%), which the authors attribute to non-additive component interactions rather than a lack of capability.
*   **Component-Level Impact:** The performance gap between AHE and prompt-only self-evolution methods (ACE, TF-GRPO) is attributed to AHE's ability to jointly evolve system prompts, tools, middleware, and long-term memory. Analysis indicated that AHE's gains were concentrated in the latter three components, which ACE and TF-GRPO do not modify.

#### 5.2. RQ2: Transferability to Unseen Tasks and Base Models

The study investigated whether the harness evolved by AHE encoded general coding-agent experience or merely overfit its optimization target (Terminal-Bench 2 with GPT-5.4 high).

*   **Cross-Benchmark Transfer (SWE-bench-verified):** The AHE-evolved harness, without further evolution, was evaluated on SWE-bench-verified.
    *   It achieved the highest aggregate success rate of 75.6%, outperforming NexAU0 (75.2%), ACE (74.6%), and TF-GRPO (74.2%).
    *   AHE also demonstrated improved token efficiency, using 12% fewer tokens than the seed (461k vs. 526k) and significantly fewer than ACE and TF-GRPO (32% and 21% less, respectively). This suggests that encoding behavior in structured components (tools, middleware, memory) is more token-efficient than prompt-based re-derivation.
    *   Gains were more pronounced in larger, multi-step repositories (e.g., django, sphinx-doc), indicating that the evolved coordination patterns were beneficial for complex tasks.

*   **Cross-Model Transfer:** The frozen AHE harness was re-evaluated across five alternate base models.
    *   All five models showed positive pass@1 gains ranging from +2.3 to +10.1 percentage points compared to their respective NexAU0 baselines.
    *   The largest gains were observed with models further from saturation (Deepseek-v4-flash +10.1 pp, Qwen-3.6-plus +6.3 pp, Gemini-3.1-flash-lite-preview +5.1 pp). This suggests that less saturated models benefit more significantly from the fixed coordination patterns encoded in AHE's tools, middleware, and long-term memory.
    *   Within the GPT-5.4 family, gains varied (+2.3 pp on medium, +7.3 pp on high, +2.3 pp on xhigh), indicating a non-monotone relationship potentially influenced by factors like task timeout and reasoning tier budget.
    *   The consistent positive gains across diverse models indicate that the AHE workspace is not specific to one provider's idioms or reasoning depth.

#### 5.3. RQ3: Analysis of Gains and Self-Attribution

*   **Component-Level Ablations:** A study decomposing AHE's gain by component revealed:
    *   Long-term memory, tools, and middleware each individually improved performance when swapped into the NexAU0 seed.
    *   The system prompt alone, when swapped, led to a regression (-2.3 pp). This suggests that factual harness structure (tools, middleware, memory) transfers robustly across tasks and models, while prose-level strategies embedded in the system prompt might be less generalizable or require other components to be effective.
    *   Components exhibited non-additive interactions; the sum of individual component gains exceeded the total aggregate gain of the full AHE harness. This indicates that stacking individually effective edits can lead to diminishing returns or interference, particularly in long-horizon tasks where components might push towards similar verification behaviors.

*   **Reliability of Self-Attribution:** The Evolve Agent's self-declared predictions (expected fixes and regressions) were assessed.
    *   **Fix Predictions:** The agent's predictions for tasks it expected to fix showed cross-iteration mean fix-precision of 33.7% and fix-recall of 51.4%. These figures are approximately five times higher than random baselines, indicating that the agent's targeting is evidence-driven and not guesswork.
    *   **Regression Blindness:** In contrast, the agent's predictions for tasks at risk of regression were less reliable, with cross-iteration mean regression-precision of 11.8% and regression-recall of 11.1%, only about twice the random baseline. This suggests the agent can justify why an edit should help but struggles to reliably foresee negative side effects.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

Agentic Harness Engineering (AHE) represents a significant development in the field of coding agents by offering an observability-driven, self-evolving framework for optimizing their harnesses. This approach presents several potential impacts:

*   **Reduced Human Engineering Cost:** AHE automates a labor-intensive and manual process, which can substantially lower the human engineering effort required to build and maintain high-performing coding agents. This could accelerate the development cycle and free up human developers for higher-level tasks.
*   **Enhanced Agent Performance:** By continuously adapting and improving the harness, AHE enables coding agents to achieve higher task success rates (e.g., demonstrated on Terminal-Bench 2) and better leverage the capabilities of their underlying base models.
*   **Broadened Access to Capable Agents:** Lowering the barrier to harness optimization could make advanced coding assistants more accessible to academic groups, smaller organizations, and educational settings that may lack dedicated harness engineering teams.
*   **Improved Efficiency and Sustainability:** Encoding behaviors and strategies into structured components (tools, middleware, memory) rather than verbose, per-call prompts can lead to more token-efficient execution. This reduces the per-call compute and energy footprint during inference, contributing to more sustainable AI development.
*   **Rapid Adaptation to Model Changes:** AHE's automated loop allows harnesses to iterate and improve at a pace that can keep up with the rapid release cadence of new base models. This prevents the "scaffolding" from lagging behind advancements in LLM capabilities.
*   **Complementary to Model Training:** The work positions harness-level evolution as a complementary axis to model-side training. It offers an externalized, auditable surface where coding-agent experience and best practices can accumulate, potentially offering a different form of continuous learning than direct model fine-tuning.
*   **Insights into Agent Self-Improvement:** The analysis of self-attribution provides valuable insights into the current limitations of agentic self-improvement, particularly the challenge of predicting regressions. Addressing this "regression blindness" is identified as a clear direction for future research in self-evolution loops.

The study acknowledges limitations, including the scope of benchmarks tested (Terminal-Bench 2 and SWE-bench-verified), the evolution operating point being fitted to a specific model (GPT-5.4 high), and the need for further development in self-modification governance to transition AHE from a controlled research prototype to a fully mature autonomous system. Nevertheless, AHE demonstrates a viable pathway for the continuous improvement of coding-agent harnesses.
