## Research Paper Analysis: DeepResearch Bench II: Diagnosing Deep Research Agents via Rubrics from Expert Report

**Date of Analysis:** February 2, 2026

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to the emergence of Deep Research Agents (DRAs), which are sophisticated LLM-based systems designed to perform complex, open-ended information gathering, synthesis, and report generation. Commercial offerings like Gemini Deep Research and OpenAI Deep Research exemplify this trend, aiming to automate multi-step online investigations and produce analyst-level reports. However, as the paper notes, these DRAs still exhibit significant limitations, such as missing key sources, over-relying on limited data, and struggling to form justified viewpoints from conflicting evidence. This gap underscores the critical need for robust and reliable evaluation frameworks.

Prior work in benchmarking DRAs can be broadly categorized. The first category focuses on **fixed-answer tasks** (e.g., BrowseComp, XBench, Deep Research Bench, WideSearch). These benchmarks are valuable for assessing an agent's ability to locate and extract specific facts or entities. However, they fall short in evaluating an agent's capacity for open-ended goal setting, complex information integration, or coherent narrative construction—facets crucial for real-world research.

The second category of benchmarks attempts to evaluate **full research reports** (e.g., DeepResearch Bench, Researcher Bench, DeepResearch-ReportEval, LiveResearchBench, ResearchRubrics). While these approaches are better suited for assessing higher-level capabilities like comprehensiveness, insight, and citation quality, they suffer from their own set of structural issues that DeepResearch Bench II aims to rectify. Many of these benchmarks rely on LLM-defined criteria, which can lead to systematic misalignment with human expert judgments and introduce "anchoring bias." Furthermore, their rubrics are often coarse, weakly interpretable, and may implicitly require the judge LLM to leverage unverifiable internal knowledge, rendering scores inaccurate or untrustworthy. Some previous benchmarks measure citation accuracy but not the factual accuracy of the cited information itself, a critical oversight for investigative research.

DeepResearch Bench II directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel benchmark that emphasizes **expert-derived, fine-grained, and verifiable rubrics**. It moves beyond LLM-generated criteria by constructing rubrics directly from carefully selected human expert-written investigative articles. This approach ensures that the evaluation criteria are grounded in high-quality, peer-reviewed content and genuinely reflect human expert judgment. By decomposing evaluation into three distinct dimensions—Information Recall, Analysis, and Presentation—and employing a fine-grained, binary rubric system, DeepResearch Bench II provides a more granular and interpretable assessment of DRA capabilities. The methodology also explicitly aims to avoid reliance on the LLM judge's internal knowledge for verification, demanding that all criteria be verifiable from the generated report's content itself.

Within the broader context of "LLM as judge" paradigms, DeepResearch Bench II contributes to the ongoing effort to make LLM-based evaluations more reliable and human-aligned. While LLMs excel at natural language comprehension, their performance can falter on complex, open-ended tasks and can be susceptible to issues like hallucination. Rubric-based evaluation has emerged as a promising method to enhance interpretability and consistency, but its effectiveness depends heavily on the granularity and source of the rubrics. DeepResearch Bench II distinguishes itself by deriving "content-bearing" rubrics from human experts, explicitly designed to avoid the need for the LLM judge to possess prior domain knowledge, thereby improving the verifiability and objectivity of the assessment. This positions DeepResearch Bench II as a significant advancement in the rigorous evaluation of complex AI agents, pushing the boundaries of what constitutes a reliable and informative benchmark.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind DeepResearch Bench II stems from the recognized shortcomings of existing Deep Research Agents (DRAs) and their evaluation benchmarks. Current DRAs, despite being LLM-based and capable of orchestrating tools like web search, frequently fall short in producing truly insightful, comprehensive, and high-quality investigative reports. They often miss crucial information, exhibit superficial analysis, and struggle to form well-justified conclusions. The current evaluation landscape fails to adequately diagnose these specific weaknesses: fixed-answer benchmarks are too narrow, while full-report benchmarks suffer from misaligned, coarse, or unverifiable LLM-defined criteria.

Against this backdrop, the authors articulate several key objectives for DeepResearch Bench II:

1.  **Introduce a Rigorous and Grounded Benchmark:** To create a new, comprehensive benchmark for DRAs that is based on real-world, expert-authored investigative reports, ensuring high relevance and quality of tasks.
2.  **Develop Expert-Derived, Fine-Grained, and Verifiable Rubrics:** To construct evaluation criteria directly from human expert reports through a meticulous, multi-stage LLM+human pipeline. These rubrics are designed to be binary, atomic, content-bearing, and verifiable *without* relying on the LLM judge's internal knowledge, thus ensuring alignment with human expert judgment and objective assessment of information accuracy.
3.  **Establish a Three-Dimensional Evaluation Framework:** To organize the 9,430 fine-grained rubrics into three core dimensions—Information Recall, Analysis, and Presentation—that correspond to the fundamental capabilities required for deep research. This framework aims to provide a granular and diagnostic assessment of where DRAs excel or fall short.
4.  **Conduct a Comprehensive Empirical Study:** To benchmark a diverse set of state-of-the-art DRAs, including both open-source and closed-source frontier models, using the newly developed DeepResearch Bench II.
5.  **Quantify the Gap to Human Experts and Provide Diagnostic Analysis:** To reveal the performance disparities between current DRAs and human experts, identify systematic shortcomings, and offer insights that can guide future research and development efforts in the field of deep research agents.
6.  **Facilitate Future Research:** To publicly release the benchmark, evaluation scripts, and all rubrics to foster community engagement, collaboration, and accelerated progress in the development and improvement of deep research agents.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to move beyond superficial evaluations and provide a truly diagnostic tool that can precisely pinpoint the strengths and weaknesses of DRAs, thereby setting a new, higher standard for their development.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of DeepResearch Bench II is meticulously designed to overcome the limitations of previous benchmarks by grounding its evaluation in human expert judgment and ensuring fine-grained, verifiable criteria. The process can be broken down into task collection, rubric design, and a four-stage construction pipeline.

#### 4.1. Task Collection and Rubric Design
The benchmark comprises **132 grounded research tasks across 22 diverse domains**, providing a broad coverage of real-world information needs.

*   **Source Article Selection:** Building on the topic distribution insights from the original DeepResearch Bench, the authors identified high-quality, expert-written investigative reports from reputable open-access journals, top conferences, and credible institutional publications. These articles serve as the "ground truth" for the tasks and rubrics. Selection criteria included:
    *   Requirement for extensive open-source information gathering.
    *   Involvement of substantial synthesis and analysis.
    *   Independence from physical experiments.
    *   Reproducibility of analyses without complex data-mining or stochastic ML models.
    *   All selected articles were under CC-4.0 or CC-4.0-NC licenses.
    This rigorous selection process yielded 132 high-credibility articles.

*   **Task and Rubric Design Protocol:**
    *   **Task Requirements:** Each task must correspond to the core research question or a significant subset of a source article, necessitating both information collection and non-trivial analysis (not just fact lookup). Time-sensitive topics explicitly define their temporal scope to match the source article.
    *   **Rubric Scoring Scheme:** A "full-rubric scoring scheme" is adopted, where each of the **9,430 total rubrics** is a binary (0/1) criterion. A rubric passes only if the model's report explicitly satisfies the requirement. Task scores are the proportion of passed rubrics.
    *   **Rubric Constraints:** To ensure verifiability and fine-grained assessment, rubrics had to:
        1.  Capture essential information or reasoning.
        2.  Be atomic and indivisible.
        3.  Directly encode target content (e.g., specific facts/inferences), not broad topics.
        4.  Explicitly verify numerical data, allowing a margin of error only for calculated quantities.
    *   **Three Evaluation Dimensions:** Rubrics are categorized into three dimensions, summarized in Table 2:
        *   **Information Recall:** Assesses the agent's ability to accurately and comprehensively retrieve and cross-validate relevant information from the web.
        *   **Analysis:** Evaluates the agent's capacity to synthesize retrieved information, derive higher-level insights, form explanations, and draw conclusions.
        *   **Presentation:** Judges the report's organization, clarity, use of formats (tables, figures), transparent citation, and accessibility for the target user.

#### 4.2. Task and Rubric Construction Pipeline
A robust **four-stage LLM+human pipeline** was developed to construct tasks and rubrics, combining automation with extensive human oversight:

1.  **LLM Extraction:** A "task-generator" LLM is prompted to extract tasks and initial rubrics directly from each source article, adhering to the specified requirements for content, granularity, and dimensional coverage.
2.  **Self-Evaluation Iteration:** To mitigate LLM hallucinations, each generated rubric is used to evaluate its own source article. If the Information Recall or Analysis accuracy falls below 90%, the LLM is prompted to regenerate the task and rubrics until the threshold is met. This acts as an automated quality filter.
3.  **Manual Revision:** Human annotators then manually inspect all generated tasks and rubrics. This stage focuses on eliminating logical inconsistencies, redundancies, and refining ambiguous phrasing to enhance clarity and precision.
4.  **Expert Review and Refinement:** This crucial final stage involved over **400 human-hours** of domain expert review. Experts ensured that:
    *   **Information Recall** rubrics are essential, consistent with the source article, and rely only on publicly accessible data.
    *   **Analysis** rubrics encode genuine reasoning/synthesis, address the task question meaningfully, and are logically grounded.
    *   **Presentation** rubrics reflect formatting requirements and encourage coherent, persuasive, and accessible reports.
    This expert curation step is vital for aligning the benchmark with human evaluation standards and ensuring its validity and reliability.

#### 4.3. Experimental Setup
*   **Judge LLM:** Gemini-2.5-Pro was selected as the Judge LLM due to its strong reasoning capabilities and stable binary classification performance.
*   **Evaluation Protocol:** Rubrics are scored independently (0/1) in batches of 50 to balance accuracy and computational efficiency.
*   **Evaluated Models:** A diverse suite of state-of-the-art Deep Research Agents was benchmarked, including OpenAI-GPT-o3 Deep Research, Gemini-3-Pro Deep Research, Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research, Doubao Deep Research, Qwen3-Max Deep Research, Grok Deep Search, Perplexity Research, and Tongyi Deep Research. These represent both commercially deployed and early-released frontier models.

This comprehensive methodology, with its blend of automated generation and rigorous human expert validation, ensures that DeepResearch Bench II provides a highly reliable, fine-grained, and human-aligned evaluation of Deep Research Agents.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art Deep Research Agents (DRAs) on DeepResearch Bench II yielded critical insights into their current capabilities and limitations, particularly highlighting a substantial gap between AI performance and human expert standards.

#### 5.1. Overall Performance
The main results, summarized in Table 3, reveal that **even the strongest models satisfy fewer than 50% of the rubrics**, indicating a significant disparity between current DRAs and human experts.
*   **OpenAI-GPT-o3 Deep Research** emerged as the top-performing agent overall, achieving the highest total score (45.40%) and leading in Information Recall (39.98%).
*   **Gemini-3-Pro Deep Research** and **Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research** were competitive runners-up, showing strong performance in Information Recall and Analysis dimensions, respectively.
*   **Grok Deep Search** excelled in Presentation (91.42%), demonstrating superior ability in structuring and communicating information, but lagged in information retrieval and analytical reasoning.
*   **Tongyi Deep Research** consistently ranked lowest across all dimensions, indicating a noticeable developmental gap.

This overall picture suggests that while DRAs are capable of generating well-presented reports, their core abilities in accurately recalling information and performing meaningful analysis remain underdeveloped compared to human experts.

#### 5.2. Dimension-Level Insights
A detailed breakdown of performance across the three dimensions provides further diagnostic clarity:
*   **Information Recall:** OpenAI-GPT-o3 Deep Research and Gemini-3-Pro Deep Research demonstrated the strongest information retrieval capabilities. Several other models formed a middle cluster, while Tongyi Deep Research showed notable weaknesses, pointing to limitations in its information-seeking behavior. The highest score in this dimension was still below 40%, emphasizing the challenge of comprehensive and accurate web retrieval.
*   **Analysis:** OpenAI-GPT-o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro led in the Analysis dimension, suggesting superior abilities in synthesizing evidence and generating higher-level conclusions. This dimension also showed relatively low scores across the board, underscoring the difficulty models face in moving beyond simple aggregation to truly insightful reasoning.
*   **Presentation:** Grok Deep Search, Gemini-3-Pro, and Gemini-2.5-Pro exhibited strong performance in Presentation, producing generally well-structured and readable reports. This indicates that current DRAs are relatively adept at the stylistic and organizational aspects of report generation, contrasting with their struggles in content generation.

#### 5.3. Ablation Studies
To validate the methodology, ablation studies were conducted on key design choices:
*   **Rubric Batch Size:** Evaluating rubrics in batches of 50 achieved the best balance of accuracy (91.75%), F1-score (89.57%), and computational cost, leading to its adoption as the default configuration. This validated the choice of batching to optimize evaluation efficiency without sacrificing quality.
*   **Evaluator Model Selection:** Gemini-2.5-Pro demonstrated the highest alignment with human annotations (91.75% ACC, 89.57% F1) compared to GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Flash, confirming its suitability as the robust LLM judge for the benchmark.

#### 5.4. Robustness Analysis
The benchmark's robustness was assessed across language and topic variations:
*   **Language Effects:** Most models (OpenAI, Gemini, Doubao, Qwen, Perplexity) showed robust performance across English and Chinese tasks (p-values > 0.05). Grok exhibited a marginally significant difference (p=0.034), suggesting slight language sensitivity. Overall, the benchmark proved largely language-agnostic for leading models.
*   **Topic Effects:** All evaluated agents demonstrated stable performance across the 22 research topics (p > 0.05), indicating that the benchmark tasks are broadly representative and do not unduly favor certain domains.

#### 5.5. Source Article Leakage
A critical concern for such benchmarks is "source article leakage," where the agent might access the ground-truth article during its research. While prompt-based restrictions were implemented for closed-source models, the authors conducted a secondary inspection. The average leakage rate was low (3.64%), with most models below 5% (Qwen being an outlier at 13.20%). This confirms that while not perfectly preventable with prompt-based methods alone, the measures were largely effective. The authors recommend stronger, tool-level restrictions for future evaluations.

In summary, DeepResearch Bench II effectively quantified the current limitations of DRAs, particularly in information recall and analytical depth, while affirming their capability in presentation. The rigorous methodology and comprehensive evaluation provide a clear diagnostic picture and establish a challenging new baseline for future advancements.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepResearch Bench II represents a significant contribution to the field of AI agents and natural language processing, with profound potential impacts on both the research and development of Deep Research Agents (DRAs) and the broader practice of automated information synthesis.

#### 6.1. Establishing a New Standard for Evaluation
The most immediate significance of this work is the establishment of a **more robust, realistic, and fine-grained benchmark** for evaluating DRAs. By deriving rubrics directly from expert-written investigative reports and meticulously validating them through a four-stage LLM+human pipeline, DeepResearch Bench II ensures that evaluation criteria are:
*   **Expert-aligned:** Reflecting actual human expert standards for quality, accuracy, and insight.
*   **Verifiable:** Designed such that an LLM judge can objectively assess compliance based on the generated report, minimizing reliance on the judge's internal knowledge or subjective interpretation.
*   **Fine-grained and Diagnostic:** Providing granular feedback across three critical dimensions (Information Recall, Analysis, Presentation), allowing developers to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of their agents.
This comprehensive approach overcomes the major limitations of prior benchmarks, which often suffered from coarse criteria, LLM-defined biases, or insufficient verifiability.

#### 6.2. Quantifying the Gap and Guiding Future Research
The empirical study on state-of-the-art DRAs yielded a stark but crucial finding: even the best models satisfy fewer than 50% of the rubrics, especially showing large deficits in Information Recall and Analysis. This quantification of the "substantial gap" between current DRAs and human experts is invaluable. It provides:
*   **Clear Targets for Improvement:** The diagnostic nature of the benchmark directly highlights the areas where models need the most attention: improving information-seeking behaviors, ensuring comprehensive and accurate retrieval, synthesizing disparate evidence into coherent insights, and generating well-justified conclusions.
*   **Empirical Baseline:** The benchmark sets a new reference point for future deep research evaluation, allowing researchers to measure progress against a challenging and well-defined standard.
*   **Insights for Model Development:** The dimension-level analysis offers specific guidance. For instance, models performing well in Presentation but poorly in Recall/Analysis suggest a need to focus on foundational information processing rather than just stylistic output.

#### 6.3. Facilitating Open Science and Collaboration
The commitment to open-sourcing the benchmark, evaluation scripts, and all rubrics (https://github.com/imlrz/DeepResearch-Bench-II) is a significant enabler for the wider research community. This openness will:
*   **Accelerate Innovation:** Allow other researchers and developers to easily integrate and use DeepResearch Bench II, fostering faster iteration and improvement of DRAs.
*   **Promote Reproducibility and Transparency:** Enable independent verification of results and methodologies, building trust and shared understanding in the field.
*   **Encourage Community Engagement:** Solicit feedback and contributions from domain experts, which the authors explicitly welcome, ensuring the benchmark continuously evolves to reflect the latest challenges and human preferences.

#### 6.4. Addressing Challenges and Future Directions
The paper also openly discusses limitations and future research directions, demonstrating a mature and forward-looking perspective:
*   **Source Article Leakage:** Acknowledging the challenge of preventing leakage in closed-source models and recommending tool-level restrictions informs best practices for future evaluations.
*   **User-Adaptive Presentation:** Identifying "user-adaptive presentation" as an open research challenge highlights a critical frontier for DRAs. Moving beyond generic formatting to tailor reports based on a user's cognitive level and background knowledge (requiring advancements in agent memory and user modeling) could revolutionize the utility of these agents.

In conclusion, DeepResearch Bench II is poised to significantly influence the trajectory of Deep Research Agents. By providing a gold-standard evaluation framework, it will not only diagnose current limitations but also vigorously drive the development of more capable, reliable, and human-aligned AI research assistants, ultimately enhancing how complex information is gathered, analyzed, and communicated.
