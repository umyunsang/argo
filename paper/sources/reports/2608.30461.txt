This report provides a detailed analysis of the research paper "From Final Artifacts to Trajectories: Retrospective Process Supervision for Evidence-Grounded Long-Form Generation."

### 1. Authors and Institution(s)

The research was conducted by:
*   **Junjie Huang**, **Weiwen Liu**, **Yong Yu**, and **Weinan Zhang** from Shanghai Jiao Tong University.
*   **Jiarui Qin**, **Di Yin**, and **Xing Sun** from Tencent Youtu Lab.

### 2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has increasingly focused on extending their capabilities towards autonomous agents that can engage in planning, tool use, and multi-step interactions. A significant factor in this advancement is trajectory-level supervision, which involves training models not only on final outputs but also on the intermediate reasoning steps, actions, observations, and revisions.

In domains characterized by objective verifiability, such as mathematics, coding, or closed-ended question answering, the generation and validation of trajectory data are comparatively straightforward due to clear ground truth signals. However, this approach encounters a fundamental limitation in open-ended, evidence-grounded tasks. Examples include synthesizing literature reviews, composing financial analyst reports based on filings, or drafting legal judgments. In these complex scenarios, a singular ground truth for validating a generated trajectory is often absent. Even when a final output appears coherent, automatically verifying the sufficiency of selected evidence, the faithfulness of claim grounding, or adherence to domain-specific reasoning standards remains challenging. Consequently, the scalable acquisition of process supervision for evidence-grounded long-form generation has been identified as an open research problem.

Existing methods for obtaining trajectory supervision typically involve distillation from advanced teacher models or reliance on human experts. Both avenues present scalability issues: teacher model distillation incurs high costs, particularly with proprietary models, while human annotation for evidence-intensive workflows is often prohibitively expensive. Furthermore, in open-ended domains, forward trajectory generation can lead to inefficient or "wandering" search behaviors, as models may lack the comprehensive foresight required for complex information synthesis.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing an alternative approach that leverages the abundance of high-quality final artifacts (e.g., published literature reviews, legal judgments) produced by domain experts. The central idea is that these artifacts, while static, can be viewed as "lossy compressions" of latent, multi-step evidence-seeking processes. By reverse-engineering these processes from rigorously vetted expert artifacts, the work aims to provide a scalable and target-driven method for process supervision, bypassing the limitations of traditional teacher- or human-dependent approaches.

### 3. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the challenges in scaling trajectory-level supervision for large language models (LLMs) when applied to open-ended, evidence-grounded tasks. While trajectory supervision is critical for developing LLM agents capable of complex reasoning and tool use, its application in domains lacking clear, singular ground truth is hindered by high costs and difficulties in verification.

Specifically, the paper identifies several key motivational points:
*   **Bottleneck in Open-Ended Tasks:** Unlike verifiable domains (e.g., mathematics, coding) where trajectory data can be automatically validated, open-ended tasks (e.g., literature reviews, financial reports, legal judgments) lack a singular ground truth. This makes it challenging to automatically assess the sufficiency of evidence, faithfulness of claims, or adherence to domain-specific reasoning paths.
*   **Unscalable Existing Methods:** Current approaches to acquiring trajectory supervision, such as distilling from stronger teacher models or relying on human annotation, are inherently unscalable. Teacher models can be costly, and human annotation for complex, evidence-intensive workflows is often prohibitively expensive.
*   **Inefficient Forward Generation:** When models generate trajectories in a forward-looking manner for open-ended tasks, they can exhibit "wandering search behaviors" due to a lack of holistic foresight, making the process inefficient or suboptimal.
*   **Abundance of Expert Artifacts:** The core observation driving this work is that while expert-level *trajectories* (the step-by-step processes) are scarce, high-quality *final artifacts* (e.g., published papers, analyst reports, legal judgments) are abundant. These artifacts are rigorously vetted by domain experts and are conceptualized as compressed representations of the latent evidence-seeking processes that produced them. This perspective transforms these artifacts into verifiable targets for process supervision.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this research are formulated as follows:
*   **Formulate Expert-Curated Artifacts as Scalable Supervision:** To establish a method where expert-curated final artifacts can serve as a scalable source of retrospective process supervision. This involves reframing these static outputs as verifiable targets for reconstructing the underlying generative processes.
*   **Develop RETROGEN Framework:** To propose and implement RETROGEN, a self-improving framework designed to autonomously generate, verify, and train language models on reconstructed trajectories. A critical aspect of this objective is to achieve this without requiring reliance on stronger external teacher models or costly human annotations.
*   **Improve Grounding, Synthesis, and Agent Performance:** To demonstrate that the RETROGEN framework leads to improvements in the grounding of generated content, the faithful synthesis of information, and the overall performance of long-form evidence-seeking agent tasks.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

The RETROGEN framework operates as a self-improving system for retrospective process supervision, structured into a multi-stage pipeline designed to reconstruct and verify tool-use trajectories from expert artifacts.

**4.1 Problem Formulation**
The paper formulates the problem as reconstructing a plausible evidence-seeking trajectory that yields a given expert-written artifact ($y^*$). This $y^*$ is considered the outcome of an unobserved expert workflow $(x^*, E^*, \tau^*)$, where $x^*$ is the task, $E^*$ is the evidence, and $\tau^*$ is the latent process. Since $x^*, E^*, \tau^*$ are unobserved, RETROGEN aims to reconstruct a surrogate executable trajectory $\hat{\tau}$ along with an inferred task $\hat{x}$ and recovered evidence $\hat{E}$ that is consistent with $y^*$.
A high-quality reconstructed trace $\hat{\tau}$ is expected to satisfy three properties:
*   **Artifact Fidelity:** The reconstructed process should preserve the essential content and structural organization of the original artifact $y^*$.
*   **Evidence Faithfulness:** Intermediate reasoning and final claims must be strictly grounded within the recovered evidence $\hat{E}$.
*   **Procedural Plausibility:** The workflow should exhibit a logical, domain-specific sequence of actions (e.g., search, inspect, compare) rather than illogical leaps.

**4.2 Artifact-Anchored Initialization**
The process begins by inferring starting conditions directly from the expert artifact $y^*$:
*   **Task Specification Inference ($\hat{x}$):** The agent leverages its reasoning capabilities to infer a plausible task specification that aligns with the final output.
*   **Rubric Induction ($R$):** An instance-specific evaluation rubric $R = \{(r_k, c_k)\}$ is induced from the artifact. This rubric consists of fine-grained, checkable criteria ($r_k$) mapped to specific target aspects ($c_k$) inherent in the artifact, serving as evaluation standards for subsequent verification.
*   **Evidence Environment Establishment ($\hat{E}$):** Concrete cues (e.g., citations, statutory references, key entities) are extracted from the artifact. These cues are used to seed an artifact-conditioned retrieval process over domain-specific corpora, resulting in the recovered evidence set $\hat{E}$.
This entire initialization phase is designed to be self-bootstrapped, eliminating the need for human-authored process traces or external teacher models.

**4.3 Trajectory Reconstruction**
Given the inferred task $\hat{x}$ and recovered evidence $\hat{E}$, the agent proceeds to reconstruct candidate trajectories:
*   **Operational Plan Formulation ($\hat{\pi}$):** The agent first formulates a high-level operational plan using a predefined abstract action vocabulary (e.g., Search, Open, Extract, Compare, Outline, Draft).
*   **Tool-Augmented Trace Materialization ($\hat{\tau}$):** This plan is then translated into candidate tool-augmented traces, $\hat{\tau} = (\hat{o}_0, \hat{a}_1, \hat{o}_1, \ldots, \hat{n}_t, \tilde{y})$. Here, $\hat{a}_t$ represents a tool invocation, $\hat{o}_t$ is the environmental observation from executing the tool, and $\hat{n}_t$ is an intermediate synthesis note. The observations $\hat{o}_t$ are populated through actual tool executions to ensure empirical faithfulness.
*   **Procedural Diversity:** To prevent the reconstructed dataset from becoming homogeneous, controlled diversity is injected across aspects such as query formulation, plan realization, outline granularity, reflection frequency, and surface formatting. This regularization aims to foster robust and generalizable evidence-seeking behaviors.

**4.4 Evidence-Constrained Verification**
Each candidate trace $\hat{\tau}$ undergoes a rubric-guided, multi-dimensional verification process to assess its adherence to artifact fidelity, evidence faithfulness, and procedural plausibility. Four complementary signals are generated:
*   **Rubric Score ($s_{rub}$):** Quantifies how well the synthesized artifact $\tilde{y}$ satisfies the self-induced criteria from $R$.
*   **Holistic Quality Score ($s_{qual}$):** Measures overall domain rigor, covering aspects like coherence, factual accuracy, or analytical depth relevant to the task.
*   **Grounding Score ($s_{grd}$):** Verifies that all intermediate synthesis notes $\hat{n}_t$ and final claims are strictly supported by the recovered evidence $\hat{E}$.
*   **Trace-Consistency Score ($s_{con}$):** Checks the internal logic of the workflow, for example, confirming correct referencing of documents in comparison steps or alignment between outlines and the final structure.
These scores are combined into an overall trace score using a weighted sum: $Score(\hat{\tau}) = \lambda_r s_{rub} + \lambda_q s_{qual} + \lambda_g s_{grd} + \lambda_c s_{con}$. Traces with failed tool executions, malformed outputs, or overall scores below a domain-specific threshold are discarded. This mechanism serves as a scalable proxy for process quality, ensuring retained trajectories are faithful, grounded, and procedurally robust without human intervention.

**4.5 Retrospective Process Supervision**
The successfully filtered candidate traces are retained for training. Each training sample consists of the inferred task specification ($\hat{x}_i$), the recovered evidence context ($\hat{E}_i$), the tool-interaction trace prefix ($\hat{\tau}_{pre,i}$), intermediate notes, and the verified synthetic artifact ($\tilde{y}_i$).
Crucially, the synthesized output $\tilde{y}_i$ is used instead of the original expert artifact $y^*_i$. This choice ensures strict causal consistency between the intermediate reasoning steps and the final generation, while the prior verification guarantees high fidelity to the original artifact. Each sample is serialized into a single autoregressive sequence, and the model is optimized using standard language modeling. This objective trains the model not only on *what* constitutes a high-quality output but also on *how* to autonomously decompose tasks, gather evidence, and synthesize long-form, rigorously grounded responses.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated RETROGEN across three evidence-grounded long-form generation domains: scientific writing, financial analysis, and legal judgment drafting. Four open-source LLMs (Qwen3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, and Olmo-3-1025-7B) served as backbone models. The training corpus comprised approximately 25,000 verified trajectories (50 million tokens), mixed with general instruction-following data. Baselines included the initial model, a forward-generation approach (ForwardGen), an artifact-only supervision approach (Artifact-Only), and public retrieve-then-write corpora (Public-Baseline). Evaluation focused on evidence-grounded generation, domain-specific reasoning, and general capability retention.

**5.1 Main Results (Quantitative Performance)**
*   **Improved Evidence-Grounded Generation:** RETROGEN consistently outperformed the Initial and ForwardGen models across all backbone LLMs and benchmarks (ALCE, ScholarQA, QASPER). Compared to ForwardGen, which lacks the expert artifact anchor, RETROGEN showed an average improvement of 4.8 points in evidence-grounded generation scores, with specific gains of 4.3 points on ALCE, 6.7 points on ScholarQA, and 3.5 points on QASPER. This indicates that anchoring trajectory reconstruction to expert artifacts leads to more reliable and less exploratory evidence-seeking behavior.
*   **Benefits of Trajectory-Level Supervision:** When compared to the Artifact-Only baseline (which removes intermediate tool interactions), RETROGEN demonstrated an average improvement of 2.7 points on domain-specific reasoning benchmarks (LAiW and LegalBench), with gains of 3.4 points on LAiW and 2.0 points on LegalBench. This suggests that explicit supervision over the procedural steps (searching, selecting, organizing evidence) provides additional value beyond merely observing the final artifact.
*   **Superiority over Retrieve-then-Write Supervision:** RETROGEN outperformed the Public-Baseline, which uses public web-grounded long-form QA data in a retrieve-then-write format. These gains were particularly notable in tasks requiring multi-step evidence aggregation, indicating that explicit tool-use trajectories offer more effective supervision than collapsed input-output mappings.

**5.2 In-Depth Analysis**
*   **Verification-Dimension Ablation:** An ablation study on Qwen3-8B revealed that combining all four verification signals (rubric, holistic quality, grounding, and trace consistency) with uniform weights yielded the best average performance. While single-dimension filters also showed improvement over the initial checkpoint, the full, weighted verification mechanism proved more stable and effective. Replacing low-scoring traces with next-highest-scoring ones (next-N replace) underperformed, suggesting that the established threshold effectively balances quality and data volume.
*   **Dynamic Agentic Evaluation on AstaBench:**
    *   **Literature Understanding:** On the AstaBench literature-understanding tasks (LitQA2 and SQA), RETROGEN demonstrated significant improvements. On LitQA2, accuracy doubled from 0.20 to 0.40, and precision increased from 0.33 to 0.67. On SQA, the global average score improved from 0.47 to 0.59, with substantial gains in citation-related metrics (citation precision from 0.47 to 0.67, citation recall from 0.33 to 0.57). Answer precision remained stable, indicating that the primary benefit was in improved grounding and attribution rather than just fluent generation.
    *   **Action-Transition Analysis:** Analysis of action transitions in the AstaBench sandbox showed that RETROGEN learned a more goal-directed research policy. It reduced the probability of repeating search actions (search-to-search probability decreased from 0.54 to 0.39), increased the likelihood of inspecting retrieved evidence (search-to-read transition rose from 0.15 to 0.23), and more frequently terminated tool use after reading (read-to-end probability increased from 0.20 to 0.55). This indicates a reduction in redundant exploration and an increase in efficient evidence processing.
*   **General Capability Retention:** Training with RETROGEN did not result in a systematic degradation of general instruction-following or reasoning abilities across benchmarks like GSM8K, MMLU-Pro, IFEval, and HumanEval. Scores were generally comparable to or slightly above the initial checkpoints, suggesting that mixing agentic trajectories with general SFT data successfully preserved broad capabilities.
*   **Case Study:** A qualitative comparison on a question about the UK Parliament demonstrated that RETROGEN produced a more compact, evidence-grounded, and non-repetitive synthesis. It linked claims to retrieved passages and covered multiple relevant aspects, in contrast to the initial model, which generated plausible but un-sourced and repetitive prose.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in training language models to function as autonomous, evidence-seeking agents, particularly in open-ended, knowledge-intensive domains.

**Significance:**
*   **Scalable Process Supervision:** The primary significance lies in addressing a critical bottleneck in large language model development: the scarcity and cost of acquiring high-quality process-level supervision for open-ended, evidence-grounded tasks. By formulating expert-curated final artifacts as a scalable source for retrospective trajectory reconstruction, the method bypasses the limitations of costly human annotation or distillation from proprietary teacher models.
*   **Leveraging Abundant Resources:** The core insight that expert artifacts represent "compressed traces" of latent evidence-seeking processes transforms readily available high-quality data into a rich supervisory signal, making existing data more valuable for agent training.
*   **Self-Improving Framework:** RETROGEN offers a self-improving framework that autonomously generates, verifies, and trains on reconstructed trajectories. This approach promotes iterative refinement within the agent, reducing external dependencies.
*   **Enhanced Agentic Behavior:** The empirical results demonstrate that this method induces more structured, goal-directed, and efficient evidence-seeking behaviors in LLM agents. This includes improvements in grounding, faithful synthesis, and the logical flow of actions during complex research tasks, as shown by the AstaBench evaluations.
*   **General Capability Retention:** The demonstration that training with RETROGEN preserves or slightly enhances general capabilities indicates that this specialized training does not lead to catastrophic forgetting, making it a viable addition to broader LLM training pipelines.

**Potential Impact:**
*   **Advancement of Autonomous Agents:** RETROGEN has the potential to significantly enhance the reliability and robustness of autonomous LLM agents in real-world knowledge-intensive applications. This includes domains such as scientific research (e.g., automated literature reviews), financial analysis (e.g., report generation from filings), and legal practice (e.g., judgment drafting or case synthesis).
*   **Reduced Training Costs:** By providing a method for generating high-quality process supervision data autonomously from abundant artifacts, the framework can reduce the economic and logistical barriers associated with training advanced agents, making such capabilities more accessible.
*   **New Research Avenues:** This work opens new avenues for research into self-supervised and retrospective learning paradigms for agent development. Future work could explore the application of similar retrospective approaches to more diverse interactive environments, longer-horizon planning, or multimodal tools.
*   **Improved Grounding and Factuality:** The emphasis on evidence-constrained verification and the observed improvements in citation precision and recall suggest that RETROGEN contributes to models that produce more factually accurate and well-grounded outputs, which is a key challenge for current LLMs.

**Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors):**
*   **Dependency on Artifact Quality:** The effectiveness of RETROGEN relies on the availability of high-quality expert artifacts that contain sufficient recoverable evidence and structural signals to support retrospective reconstruction. In domains where final outputs are underspecified, stylistically highly diverse, or weakly grounded in explicit evidence, the reliability of reconstructed trajectories may diminish.
*   **Scope of Tool-Use:** The current experiments primarily focus on evidence-grounded long-form generation tasks involving retrieval-oriented tools. Further research is required to evaluate and adapt retrospective process supervision for domains that necessitate richer interactive environments, longer-horizon planning capabilities, or the use of non-textual tools (e.g., code execution, database operations, multimodal perception).
