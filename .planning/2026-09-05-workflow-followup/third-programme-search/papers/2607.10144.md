### Research Report: IdeaTrail: Full-Process Agent Trajectories for Scientific Ideation

**1. Authors and Institution(s)**

The primary author of this research is Hengquan Guo, affiliated with ShanghaiTech University. The work was performed while Hengquan Guo was a research intern at Analemma.

**2. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape**

Scientific ideation, the process by which research ideas and proposals are developed, is characterized as a multi-stage, long-horizon workflow. This process typically involves extensive literature search, critical paper reading, tool utilization, rigorous claim checking, cross-paper synthesis, iterative brainstorming, evaluation and rejection of weaker directions, and continuous writing and revision.

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence, particularly large language models (LLMs), have led to the development of AI scientist systems capable of automating increasingly substantial portions of the research lifecycle. Examples include systems that connect ideation with experimentation and manuscript production (e.g., "The AI Scientist"), multi-agent systems for generating and refining experimentally testable hypotheses (e.g., Google’s "Co-Scientist"), and fully automated research systems operating across ideation, planning, experimentation, and writing (e.g., "FARS"). These developments underscore a growing need for training resources that conceptualize research as an extended, interconnected process rather than a series of isolated generation tasks.

In parallel, existing datasets and training frameworks for scientific ideation have primarily focused on selected outputs or decomposed decisions. For instance, "IdeaBench" evaluates generated ideas against reference contributions, while "ResearchAgent" iteratively generates and reviews literature-grounded ideas. Other approaches, such as "MOOSE-Star," decompose complex discovery tasks into sub-problems like motivation planning and hypothesis composition, providing supervision for key decisions but often abstracting away the temporally extended activities of evidence gathering, synthesis, and artifact evolution. "Agentic-Ideation" moves closer to process supervision by synthesizing tool-using ideation trajectories with oracle guidance, yet comprehensive data capturing the full, persistent research process remains a limitation.

IdeaTrail addresses this specific gap by positioning scientific ideation as a long-horizon, multi-turn trajectory. Unlike existing resources that emphasize final ideas, selected decisions, or system outputs, IdeaTrail provides process supervision that records the connected sequence of evidence discovery, tool interaction, explicit reasoning, artifact evolution, idea selection, and proposal construction. This approach aims to provide a more holistic training target for AI agents designed to perform sustained and coherent research behavior over many turns.

**3. Key Objectives and Motivation**

The central motivation behind IdeaTrail is the recognition that scientific ideation is a complex, multi-stage workflow, yet most available AI training resources either capture isolated components or present only final research artifacts. This limitation hinders the development of AI systems capable of fully emulating or assisting in the comprehensive scientific discovery process.

The key objectives and motivations of this work are detailed as follows:

*   **Bridging the Process-Artifact Gap**: The primary objective is to bridge the gap between discrete components of scientific ideating (e.g., searching, reading, writing) and the final research artifacts (e.g., proposals). The authors aim to create a dataset that explicitly records the entire multi-turn trajectory from an initial research query to a well-defined idea or an executable proposal. This includes documenting tool invocations, evidence-gathering steps, intermediate reasoning turns, and the evolution of artifacts.
*   **Enabling Full-Process Agent Training**: By providing full-process supervision, IdeaTrail intends to facilitate the training of AI agents that can sustain coherent research behavior over an extended series of interactions. This contrasts with systems trained on single-step generation tasks or isolated decisions, which may struggle with the long-horizon planning, iterative refinement, and complex synthesis required in real scientific inquiry.
*   **Developing a Reusable Data Construction Recipe**: A significant objective is to introduce a general and reusable methodology for constructing scientific-research-agent data. The "reverse-to-forward Generator–Advisor loop" is presented as a recipe to synthesize process supervision from existing, high-quality human-selected research papers and proposal artifacts. This methodology is designed to ensure that the generated trajectories remain aligned with realistic scientific outcomes while preserving the inherent uncertainty, dynamic evidence use, and staged convergence characteristic of genuine research practice.
*   **Capturing Granular Research Actions**: The dataset aims to capture fine-grained details of the research process. This includes the explicit recording of agent behaviors (e.g., search actions, reading documents), the observations resulting from these actions (e.g., tool results, retrieved information), and the updates to durable artifacts (e.g., `claim_audit.md`, `synthesis.md`). This level of detail is intended to provide rich supervisory signals for training agents to perform context-aware and grounded actions.
*   **Addressing Temporal Leakage**: A specific motivation is to prevent temporal leakage in generated trajectories. Research unfolds with a specific information horizon. By incorporating cutoff-aware tool use, the methodology ensures that agents do not utilize evidence, benchmarks, or terminology that would not have been available at the intended point in time, thereby enhancing the authenticity of the generated research process.
*   **Incorporating Researcher-Specific Context**: The inclusion of optional "researcher portraits" aims to introduce personalized context into the ideation process. By distilling prior work of an author, these portraits provide priors on domain knowledge, research approach, novelty style, and evidence preferences, encouraging the generation of trajectories that reflect plausible human researcher choices rather than generic ideation patterns.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond fragmented representations of scientific discovery and provide a comprehensive, process-oriented dataset and methodology that can accelerate the development of more capable and human-aligned AI research assistants.

**4. Methodology and Approach**

IdeaTrail's methodology centers on a "reverse-to-forward" synthesis approach, employing a Generator–Advisor loop to construct multi-turn research trajectories from existing scientific artifacts. This approach is designed to balance alignment with real-world outcomes with the need to capture the intermediate, dynamic process of ideation.

**4.1. The Forward Process Model**
The research process is conceptualized as a "forward process" comprising a coupled sequence of agent behaviors and durable artifacts.
*   **Behavioral Trajectory**: Each trajectory is represented as a sequence of turns, `τ = {x_t}`, where `x_t` includes the agent's behavior (`b_t`), tool call or action (`u_t`), observation or tool result (`o_t`), and the artifact update (`∆a_t`) induced by that turn. This emphasizes staged convergence, where ideas emerge incrementally from evidence and alternatives.
*   **Artifact Trajectory**: The durable record of the agent's research state progresses through distinct artifacts: `claim_audit.md` (evidence ledger), `synthesis.md` (cross-paper tensions and opportunities), `idea_candidates.md` (candidate directions and selection logic), and `research_proposal.md` (executable proposal). Each artifact serves a specific purpose in the ideation flow.

**4.2. IdeaTrail Construction Pipeline**
The data generation pipeline (illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper) systematically derives process supervision:

1.  **Addressing the Hindsight Problem**: A direct inverse generation (proposal `p` → trajectory `τ`) is underdetermined and prone to "target leakage," where early turns implicitly depend on the final outcome. IdeaTrail resolves this by separating Advisor-side constraint construction from Generator-side trajectory synthesis: `p → o_adv → a⋆ → ˆτ_gen`.
    *   `o_adv` (Advisor-only oracle context) is extracted from the final proposal, containing "final atoms" (thesis, method), "artifact atoms" (intermediate discoveries), "causal dependencies," and "leakage locks" (preventing premature disclosure).
    *   `a⋆` (anchor artifacts) are deterministic target versions of the `claim_audit`, `synthesis`, `idea_candidates`, and `research_proposal`, built from the proposal seed and `o_adv`. These are used by the Advisor as constraints but are not shown to the Generator.

2.  **Synthesis Objective**: The generated trajectory `ˆτ_gen` must satisfy four key constraints verified by the Advisor:
    *   **Convergence**: The final idea or proposal aligns with the anchor's core mechanism.
    *   **Leakage**: Advisor-only terms or final conclusions do not appear too early.
    *   **Grounding**: Claims are supported by observed sources.
    *   **Naturalness**: The trajectory exhibits realistic uncertainty, revision, and staged convergence, mirroring human research.

3.  **Proposal Ingestion**: The process begins by parsing a human-selected `research_proposal.md` into a structured "proposal seed" (e.g., research question, thesis, method, evaluation, risks). This seed forms the factual base for reverse planning.

4.  **Visible Direction Generation**: An initial "visible direction" (user query) is generated, providing the Generator with enough information to start research without leaking proposal-specific details (e.g., method names, final titles). Exact-term leakage checks are applied.

5.  **Anchor Artifact Construction**: The anchor builder creates the four target artifacts (`a⋆_claim`, `a⋆_synth`, `a⋆_idea`, `a⋆_proposal`) based on the structured proposal seed and `o_adv`.

6.  **High-Level Milestone Planning**: Given the hidden oracle, anchors, and a turn budget, a high-level planner creates milestones that specify what should be achieved at various points in the trajectory (e.g., early evidence gathering, middle-stage synthesis).

7.  **Turn Generation and Public Serialization**: The Generator produces one turn at a time, comprising reasoning, tool calls, and artifact edits. The public release serializes each complete trajectory as a single JSONL record, retaining turn-level details within an ordered `messages` array.

8.  **Verifier-Guided Retry**: An automated verifier continuously checks each turn or milestone segment. It does not generate content but returns pass/retry instructions. Common retry triggers include oracle leakage, pre-knowledge of paper titles, fabricated evidence (e.g., invented claims, fake URLs), or drift from the target problem space.

**4.3. Specific Features**
*   **Cutoff-Aware Tool Use**: Each trajectory is generated under a specified information horizon (cutoff date). This prevents the agent from using evidence (papers, benchmarks, terminology) that would not have been available at the time the target direction originally emerged, thereby reducing temporal leakage.
*   **Researcher Portraits**: As an optional conditioning context, "researcher portraits" are distilled from the first author’s prior papers. These summarize domain knowledge, research approach, novelty style, evidence preferences, and recurring patterns, guiding the Generator to produce trajectories that reflect plausible researcher-specific choices.

**5. Main Findings and Results**

IdeaTrail presents a dataset characterized by its scale, detailed process supervision, and comprehensive coverage.

**5.1. Dataset Scale and Integrity (Table 2)**
*   The dataset comprises 1,170 unique, long-horizon trajectories for scientific ideation and research proposal generation.
*   All records successfully parse without JSON errors or duplicate IDs.
*   It contains a total of 163,057 messages, including 38,563 assistant messages and 122,154 tool messages (each corresponding to a tool call).
*   The decompressed JSONL size is 553.5 MB, and compressed shards are 92.2 MB.

**5.2. Trajectory Types (Section 5.1)**
*   The dataset distinguishes between two endpoint types:
    *   **Proposal-extension trajectories**: 688 cases where the generated idea converged sufficiently with the ground-truth target to proceed to full proposal writing. These are marked with `_proposal_synth=true`.
    *   **Idea-level trajectories**: 482 cases where the generated idea diverged slightly from the target but still represented useful ideation supervision. These do not include proposal-specific metadata.

**5.3. Long-Horizon Context (Table 3, Figure 3)**
*   The trajectories are lengthy:
    *   Median messages per trajectory: 134 (mean 139.4, max 270).
    *   Median message-text tokens per trajectory: 73,305 (mean 78,792, max 207,064). Total message-text tokens: 92.19M.
    *   Median serialized-JSON tokens per trajectory: 110,815 (mean 116,226, max 255,865). Total serialized-JSON tokens: 135.98M.
*   The long context length is a defining feature: only 1.5% of trajectories fit within 64k full-record tokens, while 98.5% exceed this threshold, and 2.3% exceed 192k tokens. This highlights the challenge for context-window-limited models.

**5.4. Message and Text Composition (Figure 4)**
*   Tool messages account for the majority of events (74.9% of all messages). Assistant messages constitute 23.7%, with system and user messages each at 0.7%.
*   At the character level, tool observations represent 76.5% of the total text, while assistant reasoning contributes 21.4%. Visible assistant content is minimal (0.1%). This indicates that the primary supervisory signal is conveyed through evidence, intermediate reasoning, and state-changing actions.

**5.5. Tool-Use Structure (Figure 5, Table 4)**
*   Reading and retrieval tools dominate the tool-use patterns, accounting for 87.7% of all tool calls.
    *   `View` (reading) is the most frequent tool (51.2% of calls).
    *   `WebSearch` (retrieval) and `Scraper` (retrieval) follow with 26.8% and 9.8% of calls, respectively.
*   Most assistant turns (96.9%) emit at least one tool call, with a median of 3 parallel calls per turn, demonstrating coordinated action batches. The maximum number of calls in a single assistant turn is 13.
*   A significant number of trajectories (723) reuse tool-call IDs, indicating batch-local scoping.

**5.6. Supervision and Release Metadata (Section 7.6, Table 5)**
*   Every assistant message includes non-empty reasoning text.
*   Value-tier labels, designed to support weighted training, indicate 45.8% "Normal" turns, 34.0% "High" turns, and 16.1% "Low" turns.
*   Trajectory-level quality grades show 68.2% of trajectories are rated A or B.

**5.7. Topic and Temporal Coverage (Figure 6)**
*   The dataset covers a broad range of 963 unique topics. A high normalized entropy of 0.983 confirms a broad "long-tail" distribution, with 840 topics appearing only once. The ten most frequent topics account for only 6.0% of trajectories.
*   Prompt dates (representing evidence cutoff) are parseable for 94.0% of trajectories, spanning from 2018 to 2026, with 1,019 trajectories falling in 2025. This allows for control over temporal context.

**5.8. Format and Linkage Audit (Table 5)**
*   The audit reveals specific format details, such as 688 records containing proposal metadata and 723 trajectories reusing tool-call IDs. It also notes edge cases, such as two trajectories ending with a tool message and 11 tool calls having empty function names, which require robust handling in training code.

**6. Significance and Potential Impact**

IdeaTrail represents a significant contribution to the development of AI agents capable of performing complex scientific research. Its significance and potential impact stem from its unique approach to data generation and the characteristics of the resulting dataset:

*   **Enabling Comprehensive AI Scientist Systems**: By providing full-process supervision that tracks the entire trajectory of scientific ideation from initial query to proposal, IdeaTrail offers a crucial resource for training AI systems to mimic, assist, or automate multi-stage scientific workflows. This moves beyond the limitations of prior work that focused on isolated components or final outputs, paving the way for more integrated and autonomous AI research agents.

*   **Novel Methodology for Process Data Generation**: The reverse-to-forward Generator–Advisor loop is a key methodological innovation. This recipe, which synthesizes process supervision from high-quality human-selected artifacts while using hidden Advisor constraints and verifier-guided retries, offers a robust and scalable method for creating large-scale, high-fidelity process data. This approach ensures grounding in real scientific outcomes while retaining the dynamic, iterative nature of research.

*   **Rich and Granular Supervision**: The dataset provides granular supervision on various aspects of the research process, including tool interactions, evidence utilization, explicit reasoning steps, and the evolution of intermediate artifacts. This level of detail can facilitate the training of agents that not only generate ideas but also justify their decisions, manage information flow, and adapt their strategies based on observations.

*   **Addressing Long-Horizon Context and Temporal Awareness**: The exceptionally long context lengths of the trajectories (median over 100k tokens in serialized JSON) necessitate and support the development of advanced language models capable of processing and retaining information over extended periods. The incorporation of cutoff-aware tool use is critical for training agents that can understand and adhere to temporal information horizons, leading to more historically accurate and contextually relevant ideation.

*   **Promoting Robustness and Grounding**: The explicit constraints on grounding, leakage, and naturalness, enforced by the Advisor and verifier, contribute to the quality and reliability of the generated trajectories. This focus helps in training agents that produce claims supported by evidence and follow plausible research paths, rather than hallucinating or prematurely disclosing information.

*   **Facilitating Research on Agentic Behavior**: The dataset's detailed recording of multi-turn tool use and reasoning provides an empirical basis for studying and improving agentic behaviors in complex problem-solving domains. Researchers can analyze patterns of search, synthesis, and decision-making to develop more effective planning and control mechanisms for AI agents.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as acknowledged by the authors):**
Despite its significance, the authors identify several limitations. The current dataset is an initial, incomplete corpus, limited in size due to high generation and verification costs. This can result in limited process diversity, potentially leading to agent overfitting to the specific harness, tools, and protocol used. The reverse synthesis introduces a potential hindsight bias, where trajectories might appear more coherent or convergent than real-world research, possibly underrepresenting unsuccessful exploration. Furthermore, automated verification cannot fully guarantee scientific correctness or naturalness, and the data may inherit biases from human-curated artifacts. The paper also acknowledges the absence of large-scale human evaluation or downstream training experiments to validate the utility of the dataset.

Looking forward, the authors suggest expanding the number of trajectories, models, harnesses, tools, and research domains, applying stronger post-processing and human auditing, and rigorously evaluating transfer capabilities to independently implemented research agents. The primary value of IdeaTrail is therefore presented not as a definitive dataset, but as a concrete reference recipe for synthesizing robust, process-oriented supervision for AI in scientific discovery.
